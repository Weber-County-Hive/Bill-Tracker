PID Board Disclosures, and a Way to Close a PID

H.B. 422 would have made appointed PID board members post conflict-of-interest disclosures and required a district to dissolve once its debts were paid. It passed the House 66–1 and a Senate committee 5–0, then died at the end of the session.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • H.B. 422 (2026), primary sponsor Rep. Jill Koford, would have required appointed public infrastructure district (PID) board members to make, and keep updating, a conflict-of-interest disclosure (H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version).
  • The last version said those disclosures go on the Utah Public Notice Website and the district's own website, if it has one (1st Sub. H.B. 422, House Amendment 1 (Rep. Koford, Feb. 18, 2026); H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version).
  • It would have required a PID board to adopt a resolution to dissolve the district once its debts were paid, its contracts were satisfied, and its infrastructure was built and handed over (H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version).
  • It passed the House 66–1–8 on Feb. 24 and a Senate committee 5–0–2 on March 2 (H.B. 422 bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov). It was sent to Senate Rules on March 4 and never got a Senate floor vote (H.B. 422 bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov).
  • At 11:59 p.m. on March 6, 2026, the enacting clause was struck, the way every unfinished bill is closed out that night (H.B. 422 bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov; The Weber County Hive, Bills That Did the Opposite, Part 4 (11:59 p.m., March 6)).

What the bill says

Quoted from the 3rd Substitute, the last version, and from House Amendment 1, which set where the disclosures are posted (H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version; 1st Sub. H.B. 422, House Amendment 1 (Rep. Koford, Feb. 18, 2026)).

Utah Code 17D-4-202(7)(g)(i) (as amended by the bill)

require an appointed board member to make, and regularly update, a conflict of interest disclosure that includes the information and items described in Subsections 20A-11-1604(6)(a) through (n); and

The items listed in 20A-11-1604(6) are the ones on the conflict-of-interest form state legislators file (H.B. 422 2nd Substitute (Gray), passed the House; H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version).

Utah Code 17D-4-202(7)(g)(ii) (House Amendment 1)

the Utah Public Notice Website and the public infrastructure district's website

Where the disclosure must be posted. As introduced, it was the creating entity's website (H.B. 422 as introduced; 1st Sub. H.B. 422, House Amendment 1 (Rep. Koford, Feb. 18, 2026)).

Utah Code 17D-4-401(1)(a) (new)

has paid all the public infrastructure district's debts

The first condition for the board to adopt a dissolution resolution (H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version).

Utah Code 17D-4-401(1)(b) (new)

contractual obligations are satisfied or defeased

The second condition. The third is that the infrastructure is finished and transferred to whoever will own and maintain it (H.B. 422 3rd Substitute (Cherry), last version).

Utah Code 17D-4-306 (as introduced; later removed)

A principal real estate broker, associate real estate broker, or real estate agent shall

The introduced bill had real estate agents include a PID notice in advertising for homes in a district (H.B. 422 as introduced).

What happened next

  • H.B. 422 did not become law. The PID rules in Title 17D, Chapter 4 stayed as they were (H.B. 422 bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov).
  • A different PID bill, H.B. 17 (2026), on PID board meetings, did pass that session (see Where it fits).
  • We found no public statement from the sponsors on why the bill did not get a Senate floor vote. The status record shows where it stopped, not why (H.B. 422 bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov; The Weber County Hive, Bills That Did the Opposite, Part 4 (11:59 p.m., March 6)).
