Where Seized Gambling Money Goes, and the 2025 Rewrite

Two cleanup bills that matter more than they sound. S.B. 120 (2023) moved the law that decides where seized money goes, including gambling money. H.B. 21 (2025) renumbered Utah's gambling laws, gave the online-gambling crime the name used against Kalshi, and reworded who may seize gambling money.

The story in 5 steps

1. Seizing gambling moneySince 2020, gambling money and machines can be seized and taken through "forfeiture," a court process that hands them to the state.
2. What S.B. 120 (2023) didIt moved Utah's whole seizure and forfeiture law to a new place in the code (Title 77) and updated the rules. The gambling law now points there.
3. Where the money goesAfter a judge rules, the seizing agency takes out its costs, the prosecutor can get up to 20% in fees, and the rest goes to a state account that pays for crime-prevention, victim and police grants.
4. What H.B. 21 (2025) didIt rewrote and renumbered Utah's criminal code, including the gambling laws. Offering online gambling became its own crime: "online gambling promotion," still a felony.
5. Why it matters nowThe 2026 charges against Kalshi use that exact name. And H.B. 21 changed the seizure wording from "a county or municipality may seize" to items that "may be seized," and added "gambling bets" to the list.

Every step is backed by the records further down this page (S.B. 120 (2023) enrolled (final) text; Utah Code 76-9-1404, Online gambling promotion (current); Utah Code 76-9-1411, Local control -- Seizure and disposition of gambling debts or proceeds (current); State of Utah v. KalshiEX, LLC, Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, Criminal Information and Declaration of Probable Cause, Third District Court, Salt Lake County, dated Oct. 28, 2026 (AGO No. 9465862), posted by Utah News Dispatch).

Where seized gambling money goes

In simple words: this answers "does seizing gambling money help the people who seize it?" First it can pay the agency's and prosecutor's costs. What is left goes to the state, then out as grants.

From the records: a prosecutor's office that meets the law's requirements can be paid "up to 20% of the value of the forfeited property in attorney fees," on top of legal costs. The grants that come from the state account can go to "law enforcement activities" (S.B. 120 (2023) enrolled (final) text).

We did not check how much gambling money has actually been forfeited, or which agencies got grants. The state crime commission (CCJJ) publishes forfeiture reports.

Short version

  • S.B. 120 (2023), "Property and Contraband Amendments" (Sen. Wayne A. Harper; Rep. Ken Ivory): moved Title 24, the Forfeiture and Disposition of Property Act, to Title 77, Chapters 11a–11d, and updated rules on seizing, returning, selling and forfeiting property and keeping evidence. It changed the gambling law's reference from Title 24 to Title 77 (S.B. 120 (2023) Property and Contraband Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; S.B. 120 (2023) enrolled (final) text).
  • It passed unanimously in both houses and both committees (Senate 3rd reading roll call on 2nd Sub. S.B. 120, Feb. 9, 2023 (25-0-4); House 3rd reading roll call on 3rd Sub. S.B. 120, Mar. 1, 2023 (68-0-7); Senate concurrence roll call on S.B. 120, Mar. 1, 2023 (26-0-3)).
  • H.B. 21 (2025), "Criminal Code Recodification and Cross References" (Rep. Matthew H. Gwynn; Sen. Keith Grover): "reorders language into a standardized format and clarifies existing law" in Title 76, Chapters 9 and 10, which hold the gambling laws (H.B. 21 (2025) Criminal Code Recodification and Cross References, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  • It moved the gambling laws from 76-10-1101 and following to 76-9-1401 and following, and split the online-gambling crime into its own section, "Online gambling promotion," a 3rd-degree felony (H.B. 21 (2025) enrolled text (le.utah.gov XML); Utah Code 76-9-1404, Online gambling promotion (current)).
  • H.B. 21 passed the House 74–0–1 and the Senate 29–0–0 (the Senate swapped in a new version on the floor and passed it under suspension of the rules), and took effect May 7, 2025. The fiscal note estimated a one-time $43,000 cost to the courts for computer changes (House 3rd reading roll call on H.B. 21, Jan. 21, 2025 (74-0-1); Senate passage on 2nd and 3rd reading under suspension, Mar. 5, 2025 (29-0-0); House concurrence roll call on H.B. 21, Mar. 6, 2025 (74-0-1); Fiscal note, 2nd Sub. H.B. 21 (2025)).

Utah Code 76-9-1404 (current)

An actor commits online gambling promotion if the actor intentionally provides or offers to provide a form of online gambling to an individual in this state.

"A violation of Subsection (2) is a third degree felony." Internet providers, hosting companies, phone companies and ad services that only carry or store the material are exempt (Utah Code 76-9-1404, Online gambling promotion (current)).

Utah Code 76-9-1402(4) (current): the 2012 federal opt-out, kept

If federal law authorizes online gambling in the states of the United States and provides that individual states may opt out of online gambling, this state shall opt out ...

Participating in gambling, including online gambling, is still a Class B misdemeanor, Class A for a repeat (Utah Code 76-9-1402, Participating in gambling (current)).

Who may seize gambling money: the wording change

VersionWhat it says
2020, S.B. 214 (then 76-10-1112(2))"a county or municipality may seize gambling debts, gambling proceeds, or fringe gaming devices that are reasonably identifiable as being obtained or provided in violation of this part or a local ordinance" (S.B. 214 (2020), Weber County Hive Bill-Tracker)
2023, S.B. 120Same, but points to Title 77 instead of Title 24 for the forfeiture process (S.B. 120 (2023) enrolled (final) text)
2025, H.B. 21 (now 76-9-1411(2))"The following that are reasonably identifiable as having been used or obtained in violation of this part or a local ordinance may be seized and are subject to forfeiture proceedings": gambling bets, gambling proceeds, gambling debts and fringe gaming devices (Utah Code 76-9-1411, Local control -- Seizure and disposition of gambling debts or proceeds (current))
Side by side: the 2020 law named "a county or municipality" as the ones who may seize. The 2025 wording does not name anyone; it says the items "may be seized." It also adds "gambling bets" to the list. H.B. 21 describes itself as clarifying existing law (S.B. 214 (2020), Weber County Hive Bill-Tracker; Utah Code 76-9-1411, Local control -- Seizure and disposition of gambling debts or proceeds (current); H.B. 21 (2025) Criminal Code Recodification and Cross References, bill page and status, le.utah.gov). Whether this changed who can seize in practice is a question for the AG's office and the courts; we do not draw a conclusion.
