Smaller First: The Medicaid Expansion Rewrite

Voters passed full Medicaid expansion with a sales tax to pay for it. Lawmakers kept the tax but started with a smaller, capped plan that needed federal waivers.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • Voters approved Proposition 3 on Nov. 6, 2018: 555,651 yes (53.32%) to 486,483 no. It covered adults up to 138% of the federal poverty level and raised the state sales tax from 4.70% to 4.85% to pay the state's share (Ballotpedia, "Utah Proposition 3, Medicaid Expansion Initiative (2018)").
  • S.B. 96 replaced that plan in February 2019. It started with coverage below the poverty level, a per capita cap, a work and training path, and the power to close enrollment, all needing federal waivers (S.B. 96 enrolled text; Deseret News, Feb. 4, 2019, "Utah Senate OKs replacement for Medicaid expansion").
  • Kept: the voters' 0.15% sales tax, now sent to a new Medicaid Expansion Fund (S.B. 96 enrolled text). Added: a hospital assessment of $15 million a year (S.B. 96 enrolled text). Fallback: if the federal government did not approve, eligibility would expand by July 1, 2020 (S.B. 96 enrolled text).
  • It had committee hearings in both chambers and passed by more than two-thirds, so it took effect when signed on Feb. 11, 2019 (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page).
  • Federal officials authorized full expansion to 138% on Dec. 23, 2019 (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).

That is why it is not an F like H.B. 185 (2020) or H.B. 2001 (2025). It is still a D on Power and Community because it narrowed and delayed what voters passed. Average 1.5 = C−. The timeline

  1. Nov. 6, 2018 Voters approve Proposition 3, 53.32% to 46.68% (Ballotpedia, "Utah Proposition 3, Medicaid Expansion Initiative (2018)").
  2. Jan. 25, 2019 S.B. 96 made public (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page).
  3. Jan. 29, 2019 Senate committee hearing; 1st Substitute adopted (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page).
  4. Feb. 4, 2019 Senate passes it 22–7 (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page; Deseret News, Feb. 4, 2019, "Utah Senate OKs replacement for Medicaid expansion").
  5. Feb. 6, 2019 House committee hearing; amendment adopted (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page).
  6. Feb. 8, 2019 House adopts a 4th Substitute and passes it 56–19 (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page).
  7. Feb. 11, 2019 Senate concurs; Gov. Gary Herbert signs it (S.B. 96 (2019) bill status page; Ballotpedia, "Utah Proposition 3, Medicaid Expansion Initiative (2018)").
  8. April 1, 2019 Enrollment opens for the smaller "Bridge Plan," up to 100% of the poverty level (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).
  9. Aug. 2019 Federal officials tell the state parts of its plan will not be approved (Deseret News, Aug. 22, 2019, on full expansion after federal rejection).
  10. Dec. 23, 2019 Federal officials authorize full Medicaid expansion in Utah (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).
  11. Aug. 10, 2021 Federal officials withdraw approval of Utah's work requirement (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).

What happened next

  • The Deseret News reported that Sen. Christensen described the goal as long-term stability for a more limited Medicaid expansion plan. That is the paper's paraphrase, not his exact words (Deseret News, Feb. 4, 2019, "Utah Senate OKs replacement for Medicaid expansion").
  • Enrollment for the smaller plan opened April 1, 2019 (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).
  • Federal officials authorized full expansion to 138% of the poverty level on Dec. 23, 2019 (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).
  • Federal officials withdrew approval of the work requirement on Aug. 10, 2021 (Utah Medicaid, "Medicaid Expansion" page).
  • The 0.15% sales tax voters passed is still in the code and goes to the Medicaid Expansion Fund (S.B. 96 enrolled text; Hive Taxation repo, Utah tax bills 2015–2026 (S.B. 96 entry)).
