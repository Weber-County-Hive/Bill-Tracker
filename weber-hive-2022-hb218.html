Signing a Petition on a Screen

H.B. 218 set up electronic signing for some petitions, counted only registered voters, and posts each electronic signer's name and voter ID number online.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. H.B. 218 (2022) is one of them.

Short version

  • H.B. 218 (2022), primary sponsor Rep. Jordan D. Teuscher, Senate sponsor Sen. Jerry W. Stevenson, "establishes a process for the electronic collection of signatures, in the presence of a signature gatherer using an approved device" (H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF)).
  • The bill's summary lists the process for statewide referendums, party nomination petitions, and local initiatives and referendums (H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF)).
  • It "limits eligible signatures on a petition to registered voters" (H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF)).
  • For electronic signing, the county clerk posts each signer's name, voter identification number and date of signature on the Lieutenant Governor's website (H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (web page)).
  • It passed the House 68–0–7 and the Senate 28–0–1, the House agreed to the Senate's changes 71–0–4, and Gov. Cox signed it March 24, 2022. It took effect Jan. 1, 2023 (Bill page and status (Chapter 325); House concurrence roll call, March 3, 2022; Senate roll call, March 2, 2022).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF); H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (web page)).

Highlighted provisions (lines 14–18)

establishes a process for the electronic collection of signatures, in the presence of a signature gatherer using an approved device, as follows: for a statewide referendum, or a petition seeking the nomination of a registered political party; or for a local initiative or a local referendum

(H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF))

Highlighted provisions (line 19)

limits eligible signatures on a petition to registered voters

(H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF))

Utah Code 20A-7-101 (lines 409–411)

"Approved device" means a device described in Subsection 20A-21-201(4) used to gather signatures for the electronic initiative process, the electronic referendum process, or the electronic candidate qualification process.

(H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (web page))

Utah Code 20A-7-217(4) (new section, lines 1113–1117)

the county clerk shall, within two business days after the day on which the signature of an individual who signs a petition is certified under Section 20A-21-201, post the name, voter identification number, and date of signature of the individual on the lieutenant governor's website

The posting rule has an exception in Subsection (5), which we did not read (H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (web page)).

Warning shown to electronic signers

Even if your voter registration record is classified as private, your name, voter identification number, and date of signature in relation to signing this petition will be made public.

(H.B. 218 (2022) enrolled text (PDF))

In their own words

Quoted exactly from the source named. A quote does not tell you everything a person thinks; it is what the record shows they said.

A wash: electronic signature-gathering genuinely eases collection, but tighter signer eligibility and expanded public posting of signer identity work the other way

— The Weber County Hive, published grade reason, The Rules Keep Changing, Part 2 (Weber County Hive: The Rules Keep Changing, Part 2 (rubric))

What happened next

  • The 2023 code lets sponsors of statewide and local initiatives and referendums choose manual or electronic gathering before they start; if they choose electronic, signatures "may only be gathered and submitted electronically" (Utah Code 20A-21-201, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • That 2023 version of the section shows "Amended by Chapter 116, 2023 General Session" (Utah Code 20A-21-201, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • H.B. 218 is one of the 35 petition laws graded on the Ten Years of Petition Laws page (Weber County Hive: Ten Years of Petition Laws).

Bills that change the same rule

  1. 2021 H.B. 211
    made state and local petition forms and steps the same, including adding and removing signatures
  2. 2022 H.B. 218
    electronic signing in the presence of a gatherer; registered voters only; electronic signers' names posted
  3. 2023 H.B. 68
    put the rules for gathering and removing signatures on many kinds of petitions into one part of the Election Code
  4. 2024 H.B. 79
    added a way for people with a disability to sign or remove a signature
  5. 2026 H.B. 32
    online training before gathering; signers told whether their signature counted
