Notice, a Study and a Hearing Before Crossing the County Line

H.B. 115 (2021) added notice, a cost study and a public hearing before the county vote needed for a city to annex land in another county, gave mining-area and parcel owners consent and protest rights, and added a voter pamphlet before incorporation elections.

Posted Nov. 19, 2026 · Updated Nov. 19, 2026

Connected to S.B. 258 (2024), the preliminary municipality law. Both amend the same section of Utah Code.

Short version

  • H.B. 115 (2021), primary sponsor Rep. Steve Waldrip, Senate sponsor Sen. Daniel McCay, kept the rule that a city may annex land in another county only if that county's governing body adopts a resolution approving it, and added steps before that vote: written notice to nearby cities, the land in the city's annexation plan, a feasibility study and a public hearing (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text; H.B. 115 (2021) bill status page). The approving-resolution rule was already in Utah Code before 2021 (H.B. 359 (2020) 1st Substitute text).
  • A feasibility study of costs, revenue and services comes first unless the county waives it. The applicant and the county split the fee (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text).
  • Owners in mining protection areas can protest an annexation and keep their land out unless they consent (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text).
  • For incorporations: no splitting a parcel without the owner's consent, more time and a second chance for landowners to be left out, and a county voter pamphlet before the election (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text).
  • It came the session after the Legislature repealed a 2020 provision the town of Hideout used to start annexing Summit County land without Summit County's consent (H.B. 6007 (2020, Sixth Special Session) enrolled text; KPCW, Aug. 20, 2020). The repeal took effect after 60 days, Hideout approved a 350-acre annexation in that window, and in 2024 the Utah Supreme Court ruled Summit County could not challenge it (KPCW, Aug. 27, 2020; KPCW, Oct. 2020 (Hideout council vote); KPCW, June 14, 2024).
  • Who carried it: primary sponsor Rep. Steve Waldrip (now the state housing coordinator; see his Unelected Power page) and Sen. Daniel McCay. The same two carried S.B. 5004 in June 2020, an annexation bill Summit County said lawmakers were told was technical; Sen. David Buxton was its chief sponsor (H.B. 115 (2021) bill status page; S.B. 5004 (2020, Fifth Special Session) bill status page; KPCW, Aug. 7, 2020).
  • Who worked on it: the Utah League of Cities and Towns and the Utah Association of Counties worked on it between sessions to settle issues from the Hideout annexation and incorporations in Erda (Tooele County) and "a West Weber community," according to a school-district group's committee notes (Utah Association of Special Districts committee notes, March 1, 2021).
  • It passed the House 70–0–5 and the Senate 28–0–1 and was signed March 16, 2021 (H.B. 115 (2021) bill status page; House roll call, Feb. 16, 2021; Senate roll call, March 4, 2021).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text).

Cross-county annexation, 10-2-402.5(2)(e) (now 10-2-805)

the legislative body of the affected county: (i) holds a public hearing in accordance with Subsection (4); and (ii) adopts the resolution described in Subsection (4)(a)(iii)(A).

Subsection (4)(a)(iii)(A) is a resolution approving the proposed cross-county annexation (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text).

Who counts as the "owner," 10-2-401(2)(a)(ii) (now 10-2-801(12)(b)): existing law, not new in H.B. 115

the lessee of military land, as defined in Section 63H-1-102, if the area proposed for annexation includes military land that is within a project area described in a project area plan adopted by the military installation development authority

This language was already in Utah Code before H.B. 115; it appears as existing law in another 2021 bill. It sits in a section H.B. 115 amended (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text; H.B. 235 (2021) introduced text (shows 10-2-401 as it read before H.B. 115); H.B. 368 (2025) enrolled text).

Feasibility study cost, 10-2-402.5(3)(e)

The applicant and the affected county shall share equally the feasibility consultant fees and expenses.

the county clerk shall publish and distribute, before the incorporation election is held, a voter information pamphlet

How it connects to S.B. 258

  • One shared section. Both bills amend 10-2a-201.5, "Qualifications for incorporation." H.B. 115 added rules on what counts as contiguous and on areas already in a pending annexation. S.B. 258 added: "A preliminary municipality may transition to, and incorporate as, a town" (H.B. 115 (2021) enrolled text; S.B. 258 (2024) 2nd Substitute, the version passed).
  • H.B. 115's annexation rules don't reach a preliminary municipality. S.B. 258 says "Chapter 2, Classification, Boundaries, Consolidation, and Dissolution of Municipalities, does not apply to a preliminary municipality" (10-2a-509(8)). The cross-county and mining-area rules are in Chapter 2 (S.B. 258 (2024) 2nd Substitute, the version passed).
  • Landowner opt-outs don't come into play. A preliminary municipality starts only if the area "is owned by no more than three persons, all of whom consent," and at least half is undeveloped (10-2a-502(2)(d)) (S.B. 258 (2024) 2nd Substitute, the version passed).
  • One rule carries over: the voter pamphlet, when a preliminary municipality becomes a town (10-2a-510(7)) (S.B. 258 (2024) 2nd Substitute, the version passed).
  • A repeal of S.B. 258 removes Part 5 and its one line in 10-2a-201.5. It does not change H.B. 115 unless the repeal bill names H.B. 115's sections, which now have new numbers (S.B. 258 (2024) 2nd Substitute, the version passed; H.B. 368 (2025) enrolled text).
  • Weber County: S.B. 258 can't be used in a first- or second-class county (10-2a-502(2)(b)). The Legislature's June 3, 2025 list puts Weber in the second class, 276,118 people (S.B. 258 (2024) 2nd Substitute, the version passed; Utah Legislature, Current County Classifications, June 3, 2025).

"Regular new town" means incorporating under Utah Code Title 10, Chapter 2a, Part 2. An S.B. 258 town is formed under Part 5, and S.B. 258 says Chapter 2, where the annexation rules are, "does not apply to a preliminary municipality" (10-2a-509(8)). S.B. 258 can't be used in Weber County, a second-class county (10-2a-502(2)(b)).
