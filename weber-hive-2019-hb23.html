The 2019 Bill Where the House Refused a Gambling-Machine Carve-Out

H.B. 23 (2019) put sweepstakes-style machines under Utah's gambling laws and said a game is still gambling even if it shows you the result first. In the Senate, a carve-out was added for machines with age checks and 60-minute limits. The House refused it, and after two conference committees the carve-out was gone.

The story in 5 steps

1. The questionSome machines showed players the result before they pressed the button (a "preview" or "pre-reveal"). The question was whether a game like that still counts as chance, and so as gambling.
2. What the bill didIt said a game is still gambling if chance decides it, "regardless of the existence of a preview or pre-reveal feature." It also named these machines "fringe gaming devices" and put them under the gambling laws.
3. The Senate's changesThe Senate added the "preview or pre-reveal" words. It also added a carve-out: machines with an age check and a 60-minute time limit would not count as fringe gaming devices. The Senate passed it that way.
4. The House said noThe House refused. A first conference committee kept the carve-out, and the House rejected that too. A second conference committee took it out, 4 to 2. Both houses passed that version.
5. Why it matters nowThe "preview or pre-reveal" words are still in Utah's gambling definition. H.B. 243 reprinted them in 2026. An Assistant Attorney General helped present this bill in 2019, as the AG's office did again in 2026.

Every step is backed by the records further down this page (H.B. 23 (2019) enrolled (final) text; Senate Committee Amendment 1 to H.B. 23 (Sen. Todd Weiler), Feb. 6, 2019; Conference Committee Report on H.B. 23, Mar. 1, 2019 (4-2-0); House Business and Labor Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2019; H.B. 243 (2026) Gambling Revisions, Weber County Hive Bill-Tracker).

Where were the machines?

In simple words: these were not casinos. They were a few slot-style machines in the corner of a convenience store, pawn shop, laundromat or bar, sold as "sweepstakes."

Short version

  • H.B. 23 (2019), "Gambling Machines Amendments," says gambling includes a game decided by chance "regardless of the existence of a preview or pre-reveal feature in the device, contest, or game" (H.B. 23 (2019) enrolled (final) text).
  • It defines a "fringe gaming device": a device that gives a card, token, credit or product for something of value, plus a chance to win money or something of value "not substantially affected by a person's skill, knowledge, or dexterity." Devices that give the card only for a name, birthdate or contact information are left out (H.B. 23 (2019) enrolled (final) text).
  • The Senate committee added a carve-out for devices with "a mandatory electronic age verification process" and a 60-minute session limit. The House refused it. After two conference committees, the final law dropped it (Senate Committee Amendment 1 to H.B. 23 (Sen. Todd Weiler), Feb. 6, 2019; House refuses to concur with Senate amendments, voice vote, Feb. 20, 2019; 2nd Substitute H.B. 23 compared with 1st Substitute; Conference Committee Report on H.B. 23, Mar. 1, 2019 (4-2-0)).
  • Rep. Mike McKell sponsored it, with Sen. Todd Weiler in the Senate. Assistant Attorney General Steven Wuthrich and Chief Tom Ross of the Utah Chiefs of Police Association helped present it in both committees (H.B. 23 (2019) Gambling Machines Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; House Business and Labor Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2019; Senate Judiciary, Law Enforcement, and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 6, 2019).
  • The final version passed the Senate 25–0–4 and the House 72–2–1. Gov. Herbert signed it March 25, 2019; it took effect May 14, 2019 (Senate final passage, 2nd conference version, Mar. 1, 2019 (25-0-4); House final passage, 2nd conference version, Mar. 4, 2019 (72-2-1); H.B. 23 (2019) Gambling Machines Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).

What the bill says

In simple words: a machine can't get out of the gambling laws by showing you the result first. Quoted from the final bill (H.B. 23 (2019) enrolled (final) text).

"Gambling" (then 76-10-1101)

is based on an element of chance, regardless of the existence of a preview or pre-reveal feature in the device, contest, or game

"Fringe gaming device"

a device that provides the user: (i) a card, token, credit, or product in exchange for anything of value; and (ii) along with the card, token, credit, or product, the opportunity to participate in a contest, game, gaming scheme, or sweepstakes with a potential return of money or something of value that is based on an element of chance and not substantially affected by a person's skill, knowledge, or dexterity.
  • "Fringe gambling" now includes fringe gaming devices, and the gambling crimes now say they cover fringe gambling.
  • An arcade-style prize counts as a lawful "amusement device" reward only if it is worth less than $10 (the introduced bill said $5; the Senate tried $50).
  • It removed an older list of factors used to judge whether a store promotion was "clearly ancillary" to the business.

Every version, and what changed

In simple words: the House and Senate disagreed about one carve-out. The House won.

The Senate carve-out that did not become law (Senate Committee Amendment 1)

"Fringe gaming device" does not include a device that ... (ii) utilizes a mandatory electronic age verification process ... and (iii) provides a visual warning that use of the device is limited to 60 minutes and automatically terminates the user's session on the device at the expiration of the time limit.

Proposed under Sen. Todd Weiler's name and moved in committee by Sen. Lyle Hillyard, Feb. 6, 2019 (Senate Committee Amendment 1 to H.B. 23 (Sen. Todd Weiler), Feb. 6, 2019; Senate Judiciary, Law Enforcement, and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 6, 2019).

From the records: the Senate's carve-out would have let machines with an age check and a 60-minute limit stay outside the fringe-gaming definition. The House refused it twice, once on the Senate amendments and once on the first conference version. The second conference committee took it out, 4–2 (House refuses to concur with Senate amendments, voice vote, Feb. 20, 2019; 1st Substitute H.B. 23 (first conference committee, not adopted); 2nd Substitute H.B. 23 compared with 1st Substitute; Conference Committee Report on H.B. 23, Mar. 1, 2019 (4-2-0)).

The timeline

  1. Jan. 28, 2019 H.B. 23 introduced (H.B. 23 (2019) Gambling Machines Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  2. Jan. 30, 2019 House committee hearing; 11–1–3 (House Business and Labor Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2019; House committee vote, Jan. 30, 2019 (11-1-3)).
  3. Feb. 1, 2019 House passes it 69–2–4 (House 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 1, 2019 (69-2-4)).
  4. Feb. 6, 2019 Senate committee adds the pre-reveal rule and the age-check carve-out; 5–0–3 (Senate Judiciary, Law Enforcement, and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 6, 2019; Senate Committee Amendment 1 to H.B. 23 (Sen. Todd Weiler), Feb. 6, 2019).
  5. Feb. 14–15, 2019 Senate raises the prize limit to $50 and passes it, 26–0–3 (Senate Floor Amendment 2 to H.B. 23 (Sen. Todd Weiler), Feb. 14, 2019; Senate 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 15, 2019 (26-0-3)).
  6. Feb. 20–21, 2019 House refuses the Senate changes; Senate refuses to back down (House refuses to concur with Senate amendments, voice vote, Feb. 20, 2019).
  7. Feb. 22, 2019 First conference version keeps the carve-out; Senate passes it 23–0–6 (Senate final passage, 1st conference version, Feb. 22, 2019 (23-0-6)).
  8. Feb. 28, 2019 House rejects the first conference version (H.B. 23 (2019) Gambling Machines Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  9. Mar. 1, 2019 Second conference committee removes the carve-out, 4–2; Senate passes it 25–0–4 (Conference Committee Report on H.B. 23, Mar. 1, 2019 (4-2-0); Senate final passage, 2nd conference version, Mar. 1, 2019 (25-0-4)).
  10. Mar. 4, 2019 House passes the final version, 72–2–1 (House final passage, 2nd conference version, Mar. 4, 2019 (72-2-1)).
  11. Mar. 25, 2019 Gov. Herbert signs it; in effect May 14 (H.B. 23 (2019) Gambling Machines Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  12. Mar. 2020 S.B. 214 widens the fringe-device rules and adds penalties (S.B. 214 (2020) Gambling Machine and Sweepstakes Amendments, Weber County Hive Bill-Tracker).
  13. Mar. 2026 H.B. 243 reprints the definitions and adds prop bets (H.B. 243 (2026) Gambling Revisions, Weber County Hive Bill-Tracker).
