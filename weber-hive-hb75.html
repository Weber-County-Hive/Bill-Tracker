The Same Sponsors, Written the Other Way

In the same session that H.B. 61 closed off records on the Navajo Trust Fund, the same two sponsors passed an education bill that requires the state to consult tribal leaders and share data with them.

Quick glossary — click a term
State plan
The American Indian and Alaska Native education state plan. Before H.B. 75 it existed under a 2015 session law; H.B. 75 puts it in code and requires the State Board of Education to adopt it by rule.
Concentrated school
A school where at least 29% of students are American Indian or Alaska Native. Grant priority goes to these schools in rural counties with significant Native populations.
Liaison Committee
The Native American Legislative Liaison Committee, co-chaired by Watkins and Winterton. It recommended both H.B. 61 and H.B. 75.

H.B. 61 and H.B. 75 came from the same two legislators, the same committee and the same session. H.B. 61 closed public records on the Navajo Trust Fund’s investments and gave the Navajo Nation a less detailed report. H.B. 75 goes the other way: it writes tribal consultation and data sharing into state education law.

What The Bill Does

+

H.B. 75, “American Indian and Alaska Native Education Amendments,” 2026 General Session. Primary sponsor Rep. Christine F. Watkins; Senate sponsor Sen. Ronald M. Winterton. The Native American Legislative Liaison Committee recommended the bill, 9–0–2. Signed by Gov. Cox Feb. 27, 2026; effective May 6, 2026.

  • Requires the State Board of Education to adopt the state plan by rule by Jan. 1, 2027, and to present it to the Native American Legislative Liaison Committee first.
  • The plan must identify students’ most critical academic needs, rely on “the best available evidence and most recent data,” list existing programs, include a plan to hire and keep qualified teachers, and include “a process for sharing data with tribal education leaders.”
  • In writing the plan, the board “shall engage in meaningful consultation with stakeholders and tribal leaders within the state in a manner consistent with the Every Student Succeeds Act.”
  • The tribal education liaison’s yearly report must now include which entities receive grants and “the effectiveness of the expenditures of grant money.”
  • Added by amendment on Jan. 30: local plans must reflect the state education vision and mission in Section 53E-2-301.
