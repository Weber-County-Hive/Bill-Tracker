Signer Names Online, Rolling Counts

H.B. 145 had packets turned in and counted as signatures came in, put signers' names on county websites, set removal deadlines, and made paying for a referendum signature or its removal a crime.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. H.B. 145 (2019) is one of them.

Short version

  • H.B. 145 (2019), primary sponsor Rep. Norman K. Thurston, requires petition packets to be turned in, and signatures certified, "on an ongoing basis" while gathering goes on (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text; H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page).
  • County clerks must post the names of signers on the county website, and signers have set deadlines to ask that their signature be removed (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text).
  • It made it a crime to put a false date on a signature packet, and a class A misdemeanor to pay or be paid to sign a referendum or to remove a signature from one (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text).
  • The Senate first voted it down 13–10–6, then reconsidered and passed it 19–6–4 the same evening. The House agreed 50–21–4 and it was signed March 25, 2019 (Senate roll call (failed), March 12, 2019; Senate roll call (passed), March 12, 2019; House concurrence roll call, March 13, 2019; H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text).

Highlighted Provisions, lines 16-17

requires initiative and referendum signature packets to be submitted, and the signatures certified, on an ongoing basis during the signature-gathering process;

(H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text)

Highlighted Provisions, lines 18-19

requires a county clerk to post the names of initiative and referendum petition signers on the county's website;

For initiative packets received before December 1, the clerk posts "the name and voter identification number" of each certified voter "for at least 90 days" (lines 325-326) (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-305(3), lines 612-615 (referendums)

no later than the earlier of: (i) 14 days after the day on which the voter signs the statement; or (ii) 45 days after the day on which the county clerk posts the voter's name

The deadline for a referendum signer to ask for removal (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-312(3)–(4), lines 751–756

pay a person to sign a referendum petition; pay a person to remove the person's signature from a referendum petition; accept payment to sign a referendum petition; or accept payment to have the person's name removed from a referendum petition. Any person violating this section is guilty of a class A misdemeanor.

Four items from the list of what a person may not do, quoted one after another (H.B. 145 (2019) enrolled text).

The timeline

  1. 2018 Count My Vote gathers enough signatures, then falls short after a signature-removal campaign by Keep My Voice (KUER, Feb. 27, 2019; KUER, Aug. 24, 2018).
  2. Jan. 17, 2019 H.B. 145 numbered and publicly distributed (H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page).
  3. Feb. 27, 2019 House committee adopts the 1st Substitute and recommends it 10–0–1 (House Government Operations, substitute vote, Feb. 27, 2019; House Government Operations, favorable vote, Feb. 27, 2019). KUER links the bill to the 2018 Count My Vote and Keep My Voice fight (KUER, Feb. 27, 2019).
  4. March 4, 2019 House adopts the 2nd Substitute and passes it 50–21–4 (H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page; House roll call, March 4, 2019).
  5. March 8, 2019 Senate committee recommends it 4–2–1 (Senate Government Operations and Political Subdivisions, March 8, 2019).
  6. March 12, 2019 Senate adopts the 3rd Substitute, fails it 13–10–6, reconsiders and passes it 19–6–4 (H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page; Senate roll call (failed), March 12, 2019; Senate roll call (passed), March 12, 2019).
  7. March 13, 2019 House concurs 50–21–4 (House concurrence roll call, March 13, 2019).
  8. March 25, 2019 Gov. Gary Herbert signs it (H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page).
  9. May 14, 2019 Takes effect (H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page).

What happened next

  • The rules took effect May 14, 2019 (H.B. 145 (2019) bill status page).
  • Later laws kept building on the removal rules: H.B. 211 (2021), H.B. 68 (2023), H.B. 79 (2024) and H.B. 242 (2026) (Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026 (Bill Tracker)).
  • The gatherer rules grew too: H.B. 136 (2021) added badges and hourly-only pay (Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026 (Bill Tracker)).
  • In 2020, H.B. 75 changed what is posted about signers, replacing precinct with voter identification number (Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026 (Bill Tracker)).

Where it fits

How this law builds on the ones before it, from How they stack.

Signature gatherers

  1. 2019 H.B. 145 C
    new crimes, including paying for a referendum signature or its removal
  2. 2021 H.B. 136 C−
    gatherer rules with penalties; no per-signature pay
  3. 2024 S.B. 107 C+
    dropped the in-state residency rule for gatherers
  4. 2026 H.B. 32 C+
    online training before gathering; packets from untrained gatherers rejected; kept hourly-only pay

Where it ended up: Each round added rules for gatherers except 2024, which removed one.

Taking your signature back

  1. 2019 H.B. 145 C
    changed the form for removing a signature
  2. 2021 H.B. 211 C
    made state and local forms and steps the same, including removals
  3. 2023 H.B. 68 C
    put the rules for gathering and removing signatures in one place
  4. 2024 H.B. 79 B−
    added a way for people with a disability to sign or remove a signature
  5. 2026 H.B. 242 C+
    rules for people who gather removals: badges, a notice, hourly pay, no prepaid postage

Where it ended up: Removal rules grew from one form in 2019 to their own set of gatherer rules in 2026.
