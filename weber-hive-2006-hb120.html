Hold the Law Until the Vote

H.B. 120 would have kept a local law from taking effect while a referendum against it was pending. It cleared committee without opposition and failed on the House floor, 27–46.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • H.B. 120 (2006), primary sponsor Rep. David L. Hogue, would have rewritten the rule for local referendums (H.B. 120 (2006) bill status page; H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).
  • Utah Code then said a challenged local law "remains in effect until repealed by the voters via referendum." The bill struck that sentence (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).
  • In its place, a challenged local law would have had "no force or effect" unless voters approved it (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced). The bill's own summary said this corrected "a statutory conflict with the Utah Constitution" (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).
  • It also set up a certification step for referendum applications, a three-working-day deadline for petition materials, and a Utah Supreme Court route if a clerk refused to put a qualified referendum on the ballot (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).
  • The House committee recommended it 8–0–3 with no speakers recorded. The full House voted it down 27–46–2 on Feb. 13, 2006. It never reached the Senate (H.B. 120 (2006) bill status page; House Government Operations Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2006).

What the bill says

Quoted from the bill as introduced (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced). Bracketed words would have been struck. Died bills are graded on their design.

Highlighted provisions

corrects a statutory conflict with the Utah Constitution by prohibiting a local law

The sentence continues: from having force or effect until voters approve it (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).

Utah Code 20A-7-601(3)(b), struck

[The local law remains in effect until repealed by the voters via referendum.]

The rule in place in 2006 (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).

Utah Code 20A-7-601(4)(a), new

A local law challenged in accordance with this section, has no force or effect, unless and until the voters approve…

Approval would come at a municipal or county election; if approved, the law takes effect on the election date (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).

Utah Code 20A-7-302(4), new

which presumption is rebuttable only upon a showing, by clear and convincing evidence, that the application is deficient.

A certified referendum application would be presumed to comply with the law (H.B. 120 (2006) as introduced).

The timeline

  1. March 2, 2005 H.B. 274 (2005), also by Rep. Hogue, dies when the House strikes its enacting clause. It had the certification and Supreme Court steps, but not the change to when a challenged law takes effect (H.B. 274 (2005) bill status page; H.B. 274 (2005) as introduced).
  2. Jan. 18, 2006 H.B. 120 is distributed (H.B. 120 (2006) bill status page).
  3. Jan. 30, 2006 House committee recommends it 8–0–3 (H.B. 120 (2006) bill status page; House Government Operations Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2006).
  4. Feb. 13, 2006 The House votes it down 27–46–2 (H.B. 120 (2006) bill status page).
  5. March 14, 2013 S.B. 66 (2013), with a similar provision, dies when the House strikes its enacting clause (S.B. 66 (2013) bill status page).
  6. 2014 H.B. 238 (2014) shows the code saying a challenged local law "does not take effect unless and until" voters approve it, as existing text (H.B. 238 (2014) enrolled text). When that sentence entered the code was not found.

What happened next

  • The bill never reached the Senate (H.B. 120 (2006) bill status page).
  • Seven years later, S.B. 66 (2013) carried a similar provision and also died in the House (S.B. 66 (2013) bill status page).
  • By 2014, the code text shown in H.B. 238 said a challenged local law "does not take effect unless and until the local law is approved by a vote of the people" (H.B. 238 (2014) enrolled text).
