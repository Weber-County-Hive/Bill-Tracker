A Raise for One Office Became a Raise for Another

S.B. 235 started as a bill tying the attorney general's pay to the chief justice's. The House turned it into a bill tying the governor's pay to the chief justice's, which raises the other statewide officers' pay too.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • S.B. 235 (2026), primary sponsor Sen. Brady Brammer, House sponsor Rep. Jordan D. Teuscher, was introduced as "Attorney General Compensation Amendments" (S.B. 235 (2026) as introduced, "Attorney General Compensation Amendments").
  • As introduced, it set the attorney general's salary equal to the chief justice of the Utah Supreme Court's salary plus that judge's added pay (S.B. 235 (2026) as introduced, "Attorney General Compensation Amendments").
  • A House committee substitute renamed it "Governor Compensation Amendments" and tied the governor's salary to the chief justice's instead (S.B. 235 1st Substitute, "Governor Compensation Amendments"; LegiScan, S.B. 235 (2026): history and roll calls; The Weber County Hive, Bait and Switch Part 3, Case 13).
  • The attorney general stays at 95% of the governor's salary, so the AG's pay still goes up, through the governor's (S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text).
  • The fiscal note estimates $307,400 a year in raises for the governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general, state auditor and state treasurer (Fiscal note, 1st Substitute S.B. 235).
  • The governor's salary is no longer "set annually by the Legislature in an appropriations act"; those words are struck (S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text).

What the bill says

Quoted from the introduced bill (S.B. 235 (2026) as introduced, "Attorney General Compensation Amendments"), the 1st Substitute (S.B. 235 1st Substitute, "Governor Compensation Amendments") and the enrolled bill (S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text).

As introduced, Utah Code 67-22-1(1)(c)

The salary for the attorney general shall be equal to the salary of the chief justice of the Supreme Court, plus the additional compensation provided to the chief justice under Subsection 78A-3-101(3)(g).

This provision is not in the 1st Substitute or the enrolled bill (S.B. 235 (2026) as introduced, "Attorney General Compensation Amendments"; S.B. 235 1st Substitute, "Governor Compensation Amendments"; S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text).

1st Substitute, highlighted provisions

beginning July 1, 2026, sets the salary of the governor equal to the total annual compensation of the chief justice of the Supreme Court; and

(S.B. 235 1st Substitute, "Governor Compensation Amendments")

Enrolled, Utah Code 67-22-1(1)(b)(ii)

attorney general: 95% of the governor's salary;

Lieutenant governor, state auditor and state treasurer stay at 90% (S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text).

Enrolled, Section 2

This bill takes effect on July 1, 2026.

(S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text)

What happened next

  • The new pay formula took effect July 1, 2026 (S.B. 235 (2026) enrolled text).
  • The fiscal note estimated the ongoing General Fund cost by office: governor $67,100, lieutenant governor $58,700, attorney general $63,600, state auditor $59,000, state treasurer $59,000 (Fiscal note, 1st Substitute S.B. 235).
  • We found no recorded statement from the sponsors, in the records we read, explaining why the subject changed from the attorney general to the governor.
