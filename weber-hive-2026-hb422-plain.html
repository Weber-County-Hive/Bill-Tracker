In plain words · CASE 2026-422-PID

PID Board Disclosures, and a Way to Close a PID, in Plain Words

The short, simple version of our case file on H.B. 422 (2026).

Plain words · Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Want every vote and source? Read the full page

In 2026, Rep. Jill Koford sponsored H.B. 422. It was about public infrastructure districts, or PIDs. A PID borrows money to build roads and pipes for new homes. The homeowners pay it back.

  • Board members tell their conflicts: people appointed to run a PID would have had to list their conflicts of interest. The list would go on a state website anyone can read.
  • A PID has to close: once the debts were paid and the roads and pipes were handed over, the board would have had to vote to close the district.

The House passed it 66 to 1. A Senate committee passed it 5 to 0. Then it sat in the Senate Rules Committee. It never got a vote on the Senate floor.

At 11:59 p.m. on the last night, the "enacting clause" was struck. That is the line that makes a bill a law. Crossing it out ends the bill. That night it was done to 471 unfinished bills.

Our grade: A (helps). A C means a law meets what Utah law already requires. A B or A means it went further for the public. A D means a public check got weaker. An F means a check was removed with nothing in its place.
