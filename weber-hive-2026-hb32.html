Training for Every Signature Gatherer

H.B. 32 requires training before anyone gathers petition signatures and, from July 2027, tells signers whether their signature counted.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. H.B. 32 (2026) is one of them.

Short version

  • H.B. 32 (2026), primary sponsor Rep. Paul Cutler, requires anyone gathering signatures for an initiative, referendum or candidate to complete a Lt. Governor's online training course first. Clerks reject packets when the gatherer did not (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text; Lt. Governor, initiatives and referenda page (training courses)).
  • Starting July 1, 2027, clerks will send signers an email and a text, when the voter record allows, saying whether their signature counted (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text).
  • It adds a QR-code "condensed" packet option and has clerks keep verified packets at least 22 months (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text).
  • Hourly-only pay was already the rule; earlier versions of H.B. 32 would have allowed pay per valid signature, and the final version kept hourly pay (Utah Code 20A-7-104, 2024 version (hourly pay already required); H.B. 32 as introduced; H.B. 32 1st Substitute (Cutler); H.B. 32 2nd Substitute (Rep. Troy Shelley, failed); H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text).
  • It passed the House 56–11, the Senate 22–7 and the House again 70–0, and was signed March 23, 2026 (House roll call, Feb. 12, 2026; Senate roll call, March 4, 2026; House concurrence roll call, March 5, 2026).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-1-1005(4)

An individual may not gather signatures for a petition unless the individual has, before collecting any signatures, completed the online training course

(H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text)

Utah Code 20A-1-1002(3) (effective July 1, 2027)

within two business days after the day on which the clerk declares a signer's signature valid or invalid

Clerks email the signer if the voter record has an email address, and text the signer if it has a mobile number and the voter consented to official texts (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-104(1)

A person may not pay a person to gather signatures under this chapter unless the payment is based on an hourly rate.

Same rule as before; restated in one sentence (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text; Utah Code 20A-7-104, 2024 version (hourly pay already required)).

The timeline

  1. Dec. 5, 2025 H.B. 32 numbered (LegiScan, H.B. 32 (2026)).
  2. Feb. 3, 2026 House committee recommends the 1st Substitute (LegiScan, H.B. 32 (2026)). Deseret News covers it alongside signers' complaints about Prop 4 repeal gatherers (Deseret News, Feb. 3, 2026).
  3. Week of Feb. 7, 2026 The Utah County clerk turns Prop 4 repeal packets over to the county attorney; 27 packets with hundreds of signatures are flagged as possibly falsified (Salt Lake Tribune, Feb. 7, 2026 (Utah County packets)).
  4. Feb. 12, 2026 House passes it 56–11–8 (House roll call, Feb. 12, 2026).
  5. March 4–5, 2026 Senate passes it 22–7; House concurs 70–0 (Senate roll call, March 4, 2026; House concurrence roll call, March 5, 2026).
  6. March 23, 2026 Gov. Cox signs it (LegiScan, H.B. 32 (2026); Ballotpedia News, April 8, 2026).
  7. May 6, 2026 Training requirement takes effect. The course already existed as an optional one; now it is required and listed on vote.utah.gov (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text; ABC4, Feb. 4, 2026; Lt. Governor, initiatives and referenda page (training courses)).
  8. July 1, 2027 Signer notices begin (H.B. 32 (2026) enrolled text).

What happened next

  • The training course is live on vote.utah.gov; the page says anyone gathering for "an initiative, referendum or a candidate must complete training before collecting signatures" (Lt. Governor, initiatives and referenda page (training courses)).
  • Before the bill, ABC4 reported the course already existed but was optional (ABC4, Feb. 4, 2026).
  • The Better Utah Institute listed its position as "Amend," objecting that training is required of every gatherer, "including volunteers" (Better Utah Institute bill tracker).
  • We found no statement by the sponsor or a clerk tying the bill to a specific fraud case.
