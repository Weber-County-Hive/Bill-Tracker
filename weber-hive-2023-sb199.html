Two-Thirds, Then Unanimous: The Land Use Referendum Rule

As introduced, S.B. 199 would have made any land use law passed by two-thirds of a council off-limits to referendum. The House narrowed it to unanimous votes.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. S.B. 199 (2023) is one of them.

Short version

  • As introduced, S.B. 199 (2023) made any local land use law passed by a two-thirds vote not referable to voters (S.B. 199 as introduced).
  • On March 1, 2023, a House floor amendment offered by Rep. Mike Schultz narrowed it to a "municipal land use law" passed by a unanimous vote, and kept the two-thirds rule for transit areas from H.B. 462 (2022) (House Floor Amendment 1, March 1, 2023; S.B. 199 enrolled text (copy pasted by the publisher from le.utah.gov)).
  • Its sponsor, Sen. Mike McKell, said "the referendum process is being weaponized to hurt developers and cities" (Deseret News, Feb. 14, 2023, "Have referendums been 'weaponized' against housing projects?").
  • The term "municipal land use law" points to a definition section (20A-7-101) that does not define it, so whether the rule reaches county votes is unsettled (Utah Code 20A-7-101, 2023 copy (Justia); Utah Code 20A-7-101, current copy (Justia)).
  • We found no city or county that has rejected a referendum under the unanimous rule.

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill the publisher pasted from le.utah.gov (S.B. 199 enrolled text (copy pasted by the publisher from le.utah.gov)).

Utah Code 20A-7-602.8(2)(b) · lines 43–48 (enrolled)

(b) In addition to the limitations of Subsection (2)(a), a proposed referendum is not legally referable to voters for a: (i) municipal land use law, as defined in Section 20A-7-101, if the land use law was passed by a unanimous vote of the local legislative body; or (ii) transit area land use law, as defined in Section 20A-7-601, if the transit area land use law was passed by a two-thirds vote of the local legislative body.

Part (ii) was already law from H.B. 462 (2022) (S.B. 199 enrolled text (copy pasted by the publisher from le.utah.gov)).

Same subsection · lines 43–46 (as introduced)

(b) In addition to the limitations of Subsection (2)(a), a proposed referendum is not legally referable to voters for a land use law, as defined in Section 20A-7-101, or a transit area land use law, as defined in Section 20A-7-601, if the land use law or transit area land use law was passed by a two-thirds vote of the local legislative body.

The introduced version, before the House changed it (S.B. 199 as introduced).

The timeline

  1. Aug. 1, 2022 A Spanish Fork referendum on the Modera rezone, a 668-unit development, is declared insufficient: it needed 6,013 signatures and had 870 (Spanish Fork City, "Referendum Z09-22"; Deseret News, Feb. 14, 2023, "Have referendums been 'weaponized' against housing projects?").
  2. Feb. 8, 2023 S.B. 199 introduced with the two-thirds rule (S.B. 199 as introduced).
  3. Feb. 13–14, 2023 Senate committee hearing and Deseret News coverage; McKell says the process is "being weaponized" (Senate committee minutes, Feb. 13, 2023; Deseret News, Feb. 14, 2023, "Have referendums been 'weaponized' against housing projects?").
  4. March 1, 2023 House narrows it to unanimous votes and passes it 55–15–5 (House Floor Amendment 1, March 1, 2023; House 3rd reading roll call, March 1, 2023).
  5. March 2, 2023 Senate agrees 16–9–4 (Senate concurrence roll call, March 2, 2023).
  6. March 23, 2023 Gov. Cox signs it (S.B. 199 (2023) bill status page).
  7. March 27, 2023 Mountain Green residents file a referendum on a Morgan County rezone passed 4–1 (Morgan County News, May 15, 2023; Morgan County News, March 30, 2023).
  8. May 3, 2023 Takes effect (S.B. 199 enrolled text (copy pasted by the publisher from le.utah.gov)).

What happened next

  • Mountain Green (Morgan County), 2023: the commission approved a rezone 4–1. The Morgan County News wrote that the lone no vote "appears to have allowed the petition to move forward" (Morgan County News, May 15, 2023; Morgan County News, March 30, 2023). The outcome of that petition was not found.
  • ULCT later summed up the law as: "Made land use regulations that a local government approved unanimously to be nonreferable" (ULCT, "Charting Utah's Growth Legislation," Sept. 2023).
  • Park City, 2026: a letter to the editor about the Bonanza Park decision quoted the unanimous-vote language; no referendum application was found (Park Record, letter to the editor, July 11, 2026).
  • We found no city or county lawyer rejecting a referendum under the unanimous rule (searched Oct. 19, 2026).

Where it fits

How this law builds on the ones before it, from How they stack.

Local land-use referendums

  1. 2016 H.B. 10 B−
    widened "local law" so residents can refer more kinds of local action
  2. 2019 H.B. 119 D
    added a step to decide whether a measure is "legally referable to voters," and land-use rules
  3. 2021 H.B. 23 C+
    changed the definition of a land-use law and same-year ballot timing
  4. 2022 H.B. 462 D
    some transit-area land-use laws not referable after a two-thirds council vote; higher signature counts for the rest
  5. 2023 S.B. 199 D
    introduced as two-thirds for any land-use law; passed as "municipal" land-use laws not referable after a unanimous vote
  6. 2024 S.B. 100 C+
    opened some revenue-bond decisions to local referendum

Where it ended up: It started wider in 2016. Since 2019, three of these laws (H.B. 119, H.B. 462 and S.B. 199) set conditions that keep some land-use laws off the ballot. S.B. 100 added a kind of decision that is not a land-use law.
