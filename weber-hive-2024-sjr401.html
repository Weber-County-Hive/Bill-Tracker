Amendment D: Six Weeks After the Ruling

A proposed constitutional amendment on the Legislature's power over voter-passed laws, written in a one-day special session and voided by the courts.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. S.J.R. 401 (Amendment D) (2024) is one of them.

Short version

  • On July 11, 2024, the Utah Supreme Court ruled in the Prop 4 case that the Legislature's power to change certain voter-passed laws has limits (Utah News Dispatch, Oct. 24, 2024).
  • Six weeks later, on Aug. 21, 2024, lawmakers passed S.J.R. 401, Amendment D, in a special session with no committee hearing: Senate 20–8, House 54–21 (S.J.R. 401 bill status page; Senate roll call, Aug. 21, 2024; House roll call, Aug. 21, 2024).
  • It said the people's lawmaking power "does not limit or preclude" the Legislature "amending, enacting, or repealing a law," and made that part retroactive (S.J.R. 401 enrolled (XML)).
  • A judge voided it Sept. 12, 2024. The Utah Supreme Court agreed: the required newspaper notice was not published, and the ballot language was misleading (Salt Lake Tribune, Sept. 12, 2024; KSL, Sept. 25, 2024 (Supreme Court order); FOX 13, Oct. 24, 2024).
  • It stayed on the 2024 ballot, but votes on it were not counted (KSL, Sept. 25, 2024 (Supreme Court order)).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled resolution (S.J.R. 401 enrolled (XML)).

Proposed Article VI, Section 1(4)

Notwithstanding any other provision of this Constitution, the people's exercise of their Legislative power as provided in Subsection (2) does not limit or preclude the exercise of Legislative power, including through amending, enacting, or repealing a law, by the Legislature, or by a law making body of a county, city, or town, on behalf of the people whom they are elected to represent.

(S.J.R. 401 enrolled (XML))

Proposed Article VI, Section 1(3)

(a) Foreign individuals, entities, or governments may not, directly or indirectly, influence, support, or oppose an initiative or a referendum.

(S.J.R. 401 enrolled (XML))

Section 5, retrospective operation

The actions affecting Article I, Section 2 and Article VI, Section 1, Subsection (4) have retrospective operation.

(S.J.R. 401 enrolled (XML))

The timeline

  1. July 11, 2024 Utah Supreme Court rules in League of Women Voters v. Utah State Legislature (Utah News Dispatch, Oct. 24, 2024).
  2. Aug. 19, 2024 Senate President Adams and Speaker Schultz call a special session (Utah Senate release, Aug. 19, 2024).
  3. Aug. 21, 2024 Amendment D passes both chambers the same evening (Senate roll call, Aug. 21, 2024; House roll call, Aug. 21, 2024).
  4. Sept. 12, 2024 Third District Judge Dianna Gibson voids it: "Amendment D is void and shall be given no effect" (Salt Lake Tribune, Sept. 12, 2024).
  5. Sept. 25, 2024 Utah Supreme Court affirms (KSL, Sept. 25, 2024 (Supreme Court order)).
  6. Oct. 24, 2024 Full opinion by Justice Diana Hagen: "Amendment D changes rather than clarifies" (Utah News Dispatch, Oct. 24, 2024; FOX 13, Oct. 24, 2024).
  7. Nov. 5, 2024 On the ballot, but votes not counted (KSL, Sept. 25, 2024 (Supreme Court order)).
  8. 2025 Lawmakers pass Amendment A (H.J.R. 10), which would remove the newspaper rule that was one reason Amendment D was voided (Transparency: The Amendments on Your Ballot, Part 1).

What happened next

  • The Utah Constitution was not changed (Utah News Dispatch, Oct. 24, 2024).
  • In 2025, lawmakers passed Amendment A (H.J.R. 10), on this year's ballot, which would remove the newspaper notice rule. See The Amendments on Your Ballot (Transparency: The Amendments on Your Ballot, Part 1).
  • In February 2026, ABC4 reported lawmakers were considering another special session on initiatives. Senate President Adams: "My position on initiatives is very clear, I believe they should be on the same equal footing as a statue" (ABC4, Feb. 25, 2026).
