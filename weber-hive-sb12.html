CASE 2026-012

S.B. 12 · 2026 General Session

Sixteen Extensions, Four Exceptions

Introduced as a single-issue bill extending one committee's sunset date, a substitute quietly grew it into an omnibus extending roughly sixteen more programs — while leaving Utah's Air Conservation Act, Safe Drinking Water Act, Water Quality Act, and Solid and Hazardous Waste Act to expire on schedule, in the same section, untouched.

Chief Sponsor: Sen. Todd WeilerHouse Sponsor: Rep. Andrew Stoddard Effective: May 6, 2026 Became Law

New Here? Start Simple

Utah's laws come with built-in expiration dates, and the Legislature has to vote to extend them or they go away.

This bill started out doing one thing: extending the expiration date of the law that lets Utahns use mediation instead of a lawsuit. Then, before it ever reached a floor vote in its final form, a substitute grew it into a much bigger bill — extending roughly sixteen other programs, boards, and licensing laws by years.

In that same section of law, sitting right next to everything that got extended, four environmental protection laws — covering air quality, drinking water, water quality, and hazardous waste — were left completely alone. They're still set to expire on schedule.

Every recorded vote on this bill, in both the House and Senate, happened while it was still the small, one-issue bill. The expanded version became law without anyone casting a recorded vote on what it had grown into.

How This Could Have Actually Served the Public

A sunset-extension bill isn't inherently a problem — the issue here is scope and process. A version that served the public better would have:

  • Required a fresh committee hearing on the expanded, roughly 16-item version, instead of letting a bill that grew about sixteen times in scope ride on votes cast for the one-item original
  • Either extended the four environmental statutes along with everything else in the same section, or explained on the record why they were left to expire while so much else nearby was renewed
  • Kept the bill's title accurate to its final scope, so anyone reading “Sunset and Repeal Date Amendments” could tell what actually changed without comparing three separate substitutes
