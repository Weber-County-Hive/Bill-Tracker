CASE 2026-242

S.B. 242 · 2026 General Session

The Bill Behind the Tax — Transportation Amendments

Utah's House called it "the session's most wide-ranging transit bill." Buried in its fourth substitute is the exact code section that lets Weber County collect a new 0.2% sales tax starting this fall — and missing from that same substitute is a consumer privacy protection that existed when the bill was introduced.

Chief Sponsor: Sen. Wayne Harper House Sponsor: Rep. Kay Christofferson Signed: Mar 25, 2026 Became Law

What the bill actually does

S.B. 242 went through at least four substitutes (introduced, S01, S02, S04) before passing. On its face, it's a broad transportation package: it requires commercial EVs over 6,001 lbs gross combined weight to pay a road usage charge with a higher cap; exempts UDOT from certain real-property conveyance requirements; exempts UDOT transit construction materials from sales tax; clarifies land-use rules near FrontRunner stations to speed the double-tracking project; and lets transit buses cross lane lines while "loading or unloading passengers, or making an operational adjustment."

Two provisions are attributed to House sponsor Christofferson by name in the House's own 2026 session recap: ending the program that let EVs use HOV lanes (tied to an expired federal policy), and routing corridor-preservation funding directly from the state to local governments.

The Weber County tax connection

Section 9 of this bill amends Utah Code §59-12-2220 — the exact statute governing county-option sales tax for highways and transit — setting the 0.2% rate and the distribution formulas by county classification and transit-district status.

That's the section behind Weber County's new "5th 5th" local-option sales tax: 0.2%, effective October 1, 2026, projected to raise roughly $13.1 million a year. Weber County Commissioner Gage Froerer publicly described the tax as "an alternative to boosting property taxes." The specific language enabling it is what KSL's own coverage referred to as language "inserted in a state transportation bill" — this bill, confirmed directly against the amended code section.

The deletion no record explains

A consumer privacy protection present in the introduced and S01 versions of the bill was deleted by S04 — the version that actually became law. The removed language would have barred towing dispatch vendors from selling a vehicle owner's personal information, sharing it with third parties, using it beyond the tow itself, or retaining it longer than administratively necessary.

That protection does not exist in the enacted law. The public legislative record — bill text, committee minutes, floor debate — does not explain why it was removed between S01 and S04. It's the kind of provision a reader following only the bill's stated purpose (transportation funding and governance) would have no reason to expect was ever in play, let alone struck.

Real, organized opposition — that didn't stop it

At the Senate Transportation, Public Utilities, Energy & Technology Committee hearing, a genuine coalition testified against the bill: Dave Iltis (President, Cycling Utah), Julia Lyon (President, Wasatch Elementary PTA), Julian Jurkoic (Treasurer, Sweet Streets Salt Lake City), and six named residents. Only one witness — Salt Lake City Council Member Alejandro Puy — spoke in favor.

One plausible, checkable explanation for that opposition: the bill exempts transit buses from lane-discipline rules specifically while loading, unloading, or making an operational adjustment — meaning a bus can legally block a bike lane during that window. Not confirmed as the reason bike-safety advocates opposed the bill, but a specific candidate worth naming.

Opposition was genuinely cross-partisan, not party-line. House passage (as Substitute 4) was 55-14-6 — most Nays were Democrats, but four Republicans crossed over (Abbott, Gwynn, Ward, Welton). On the Senate concurrence vote (18-9-2), Nays mixed both parties: Democrats Blouin, Escamilla, Kwan, Plumb, and Riebe alongside Republicans Balderree, Brammer, Buss, and Pitcher.
