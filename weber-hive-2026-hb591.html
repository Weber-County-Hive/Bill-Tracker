The Nuisance Law That Kept One Lawsuit for the AG

H.B. 591 ended most common-law nuisance lawsuits in Utah, kept one that only the Attorney General can bring, and said permitted or licensed activities are not a nuisance. It passed in the last hour of the 2026 session. Seven months later, the AG used that one lawsuit against Kalshi.

The story in 5 steps

1. What's a nuisance?If your neighbor's dog barks all night, every night, that's a nuisance. If a factory's smoke covers the whole town, that's a nuisance too. For hundreds of years, a judge could make it stop if you, your town or the state asked.
2. What the new law didIt changed who is allowed to ask. You and your neighbors still can, but the rules are much stricter. Your town can only if the whole problem is inside town lines. If the government gave something a permit, it doesn't count as a nuisance anymore. Only the Attorney General keeps the old, easier way.
3. The part the House didn't seeThe House voted yes first, on a bill where nobody kept the old way, not even the Attorney General. Then, while the bill was in the Senate, a new version added the special power for him. That version is listed under the House sponsor, Rep. Jack. It was going to end in 2035; later they took the end date out.
4. The last nightJust before midnight, the bill went back to the House. The sponsor said the change was about the governor. He did not mention the Attorney General's special power. The House said yes at 11:26 PM, 34 minutes before they had to stop.
5. What happened nextIn October, the Attorney General used that special power to sue Kalshi, a betting app.
The moneyThe Attorney General's campaign gave $2,000 to the Senate sponsor and $300 to the House sponsor. A lawyers' group that was against the bill gave the Senate sponsor $1,750. The records show the gifts and dates, not why anyone gave.
Who pays the lawyersIn June, the AG hired a Chicago law firm for gambling cases. It gets a share of any money the state wins, and the state pays back its costs.

Short version

  • H.B. 591 (2026), "Nuisance Amendments," ends common-law (court-made) nuisance lawsuits in Utah, with one exception: the Attorney General may still bring one when he finds "substantial ongoing harm to a significant number of the citizens of Utah." It says "A person other than the attorney general may not bring a common law civil right of action" (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text).
  • Written rules replace the court-made ones. A city or county may sue only over a nuisance entirely inside its borders. A person may sue only with a "special injury," which leaves out harm based on the spiritual, cultural or emotional meaning of a lake, river, park or road, and money spent responding (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text).
  • Anything "authorized, approved, licensed, or mandated" by a statute, ordinance, permit, license or rule is not a public or private nuisance under the new definitions. The Utah Association for Justice asked the House committee to take that out. It stayed in (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text; House Public Utilities and Energy Committee video, Feb. 25, 2026; House Public Utilities and Energy Committee minutes, Feb. 25, 2026).
  • Rep. Colin Jack and Sen. Brady Brammer sponsored it. AG Derek Brown and the U.S. Chamber Institute for Legal Reform presented it with Jack in both committees (H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; House Public Utilities and Energy Committee minutes, Feb. 25, 2026; Senate Judiciary, Law Enforcement, and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Mar. 4, 2026). It was numbered Feb. 20, two weeks before the session ended (H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  • The AG's exception was not in the bill the House passed. As introduced and as passed by the House on Feb. 27, H.B. 591 ended common-law nuisance lawsuits for everyone, the AG included. The AG-only exception first appears in the 1st Substitute, dated March 3, after the bill reached the Senate; that version is listed under Rep. Jack (H.B. 591 as introduced; 1st Substitute H.B. 591 (file dated Mar. 3, 2026, 7:09 PM); H.B. 591 bill text files (introduced, 1st, 2nd and 3rd Substitutes, enrolled), le.utah.gov, compared word by word Nov. 4, 2026; House 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 27, 2026 (60-9-6)).
  • On the last night, March 6, the Senate swapped in a new version and a floor amendment by voice vote and passed it 21–6–2 at 10:55 PM under suspension of the rules. The House agreed at 11:26 PM, 47–14–14, after Rep. Jack described the Senate's changes as a clarification about the governor; he did not mention the AG exception (House floor video, Mar. 6, 2026 (concurrence on 3rd Sub. H.B. 591)). Gov. Cox signed it March 25; it took effect May 6, the same day as H.B. 243 (Senate floor: motion to substitute 3rd Sub., voice vote, Mar. 6, 2026, 10:52 PM; Senate floor amendment #1, voice vote, Mar. 6, 2026, 10:53 PM; Senate passage on 2nd and 3rd reading under suspension of the rules, Mar. 6, 2026, 10:55 PM (21-6-2); House concurrence roll call, Mar. 6, 2026, 11:26 PM (47-14-14); H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  • Brown's campaign gave Brammer $2,000 on June 3 and Jack $300 on Oct. 27, as filed. The Utah Association for Justice, which opposed the bill, gave Brammer $1,000 on Jan. 16 and $750 on June 5 (disclosures.utah.gov, candidate and PAC filings and 'Download Data by Year' files, 2018-2026; disclosures.utah.gov Advanced Search, 2025 and 2026 contributions, searched for 'Association for Justice' and 'Chamber' (read Nov. 4, 2026)). New to this? "Nuisance," explained simply

A private nuisanceYour neighbor's dog barks all night, every night, and you can't sleep. It hurts you and your home. For hundreds of years, courts have let people sue over that.
A public nuisanceA factory's smoke covers a whole town, or a business blocks a public road. It hurts everyone. Cities, counties, the state, and sometimes a person hurt in a special way could sue to stop it.
"Common law"Rules judges built up case by case, not written by lawmakers. Before H.B. 591, much of Utah's nuisance law worked this way.
What H.B. 591 didIt replaced the judge-made rules with written ones. Under the written rules, fewer people can sue, and anything a permit or license allows is not a nuisance. One person keeps the old judge-made power: the Attorney General.
Why people argued about itSupporters said lawsuits were being used to make policy that lawmakers should make. Opponents said permitted polluters and big companies get a shield, and neighbors lose a tool.
How it touches betting appsIn October, the AG declared online gambling a public nuisance and sued Kalshi using the one power this bill kept for him.

What the bill says

Quoted from the final, enrolled bill (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text).

78B-6a-102, the AG exception

substantial ongoing harm to a significant number of the citizens of Utah

The AG may bring a common-law nuisance action when he determines this, acting for the state or as parens patriae (on behalf of the people).

78B-6a-102(3)

A person other than the attorney general may not bring a common law civil right of action

Definitions: what is not a nuisance

authorized, approved, licensed, or mandated by statute, ordinance, regulation, permit, license, order, rule…

An action or condition that is lawful, or approved this way, is not a "public nuisance" or "private nuisance."

  • 78B-6a-102(4): the governor may direct the AG to bring an action.
  • 78B-6a-102(6): the AG reports to the Legislature's Judiciary Interim Committee before Oct. 1, 2029, and every four years after; the committee decides whether to recommend repealing the AG exception and the bar on others.
  • 78B-6a-202: a city, town or county may sue only if the nuisance is entirely within its jurisdiction; otherwise only the AG.
  • 78B-6a-203: a person may sue over a public nuisance only with a "special injury." That excludes harm based on the spiritual, cultural or emotional significance of lakes, rivers, parks and roads, and money spent responding.

How wide is the AG's power?

In simple words: the law took the old way of stopping a nuisance away from everyone else and kept it for one person. Here is what limits him, and what doesn't, from the words of the law (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text; H.B. 591 bill text files (introduced, 1st, 2nd and 3rd Substitutes, enrolled), le.utah.gov, compared word by word Nov. 4, 2026).

What the law saysWhat that means
He may use it when "the attorney general determines" there is "substantial ongoing harm to a significant number of the citizens of Utah."He decides. The bill does not define "substantial," "ongoing" or "significant number."
It is not limited to a topic.It can be used on anything he finds meets that test: gambling apps, or something else.
He must act for the state, a state office or a town, or for the people of Utah (parens patriae).He brings it for the state or the people of Utah, not for a private party.
The governor may direct him to bring one.The governor can push for a case.
He reports to the Judiciary Interim Committee before Oct. 1, 2029, then every four years.The first look by lawmakers comes about three and a half years after the law took effect. The 2035 end date was taken out.
(Courts)A judge still decides whether he wins each case. The law limits who may ask, not what a judge may decide.

Supporters said the change stops courts from being used to make policy; Brammer told the Senate policy decisions "belong to the people and their elected representatives" (Senate floor video, Mar. 6, 2026 (3rd Sub. H.B. 591); House Public Utilities and Energy Committee video, Feb. 25, 2026). The bill keeps the old way for one elected official. Readers can weigh both.

The last hour

In simple words: lawmakers must finish by midnight on the last night. Normally a bill is read and voted on over different days. "Suspension of the rules" means they skipped that and did it all at once.

From the records: the version of H.B. 591 that became law was put in front of the Senate, amended and passed in about three minutes, under suspension of the rules, which lets the Senate vote on 2nd and 3rd reading at once. The House had it for about half an hour before agreeing. The records do not show any public hearing on the final text (Senate floor: motion to substitute 3rd Sub., voice vote, Mar. 6, 2026, 10:52 PM; Senate floor amendment #1, voice vote, Mar. 6, 2026, 10:53 PM; Senate passage on 2nd and 3rd reading under suspension of the rules, Mar. 6, 2026, 10:55 PM (21-6-2); House concurrence roll call, Mar. 6, 2026, 11:26 PM (47-14-14)).

Who is paying the lawyers?

In simple words: the AG's office hired a private law firm from Chicago to help with gambling cases. The firm gets paid a share of any money the state wins. If the state wins nothing, the firm gets no share. The state pays back the firm's costs, like travel and experts, when they are agreed to ahead of time. Everything below is from the posted contract (Legal Services Agreement GN26-16, Gambling Prohibition Enforcement, Office of the Utah Attorney General and Edelson PC, effective June 22, 2026 (posted on the AG's website; 22 pages, read as rendered pages Nov. 4, 2026)).

What the contract saysWhere
The AG's office hired Edelson PC, 350 North LaSalle St., Chicago, for "Gambling Cases." Effective June 22, 2026, seven weeks after H.B. 591 and H.B. 243 took effect. Signed by Douglas Crapo for the AG's office (June 22) and Jimmy Rock for Edelson (June 23). Contract number GN26-16, chosen through a request for proposalsPage 1, signature page
"Gambling Cases" means cases against makers of online sweepstakes games, social casinos or promotional contests "that allow a user to bet upon the outcome of a future contingent event not under his or her control," where a prize can be redeemed for something of valueSection 1.4(E)
"Unlawful Conduct" includes violations of "state and federal constitutional, statutory, common law, or equitable claims"Section 1.4(N)
The firm's share is capped at 25% of the first $10 million, stepping down to 10% above $20 million, with a top limit of $50 million. "Actual percentages will be based upon the Lawyers' Cost Proposal," which is not attached to the posted contractSection 6.2
The share is figured on money paid to the state "for restitution, disgorgement, or actual damages, including any fines or civil penalties"Section 6.3
Costs and expenses are "reimbursed by the OAG at no more than 100% of the amount actually incurred." The firm may be paid "only from designated funds or accounts identified by the OAG"Sections 5.1, 8.4
If the AG ends the contract, the firm still gets a share of later wins, from 10% of its fee once pleadings start up to 100% after trialSection 6.7
The contract runs until every gambling case is resolvedSection 11.1
The AG keeps control: no lawsuit may be filed and no settlement made without the state's written approvalSection 2.4
Side by side: the contract counts "fines or civil penalties" toward the firm's share (Section 6.3). Utah Code 67-5-33(5)(c) says a contingent fee "may not be based on the imposition or amount of a penalty or civil fine." The same law says a contract term that does not match it is invalid unless the AG, the state treasurer and the state auditor approve it by majority before signing (Legal Services Agreement GN26-16, Gambling Prohibition Enforcement, Office of the Utah Attorney General and Edelson PC, effective June 22, 2026 (posted on the AG's website; 22 pages, read as rendered pages Nov. 4, 2026); Utah Code 67-5-33, Contingent fee contracts (le.utah.gov)). We did not find a record of such an approval. Readers can ask.

Followed the law: the contract was posted on the AG's website June 29, within the five business days Utah Code 67-5-33(7) requires. The same law also requires posting the AG's written finding that the contract is "cost-effective and in the public interest." The contract says that finding was made. We did not find it posted: we searched the AG website's file library for "determination," "GN26," "contingen" and "gambling" on Nov. 4, and the 22-page contract file does not include it (Legal Services Agreement GN26-16, Gambling Prohibition Enforcement, Office of the Utah Attorney General and Edelson PC, effective June 22, 2026 (posted on the AG's website; 22 pages, read as rendered pages Nov. 4, 2026); Utah Code 67-5-33, Contingent fee contracts (le.utah.gov)).

Not confirmed: whether Edelson PC works on the Kalshi lawsuit (the contract's definition names sweepstakes and social casinos; the news reports we read did not name outside lawyers), and what the state has paid in costs so far. The AG must report contingent-fee contracts and fees paid to legislative leaders each year between July 1 and Sept. 1 (Utah Code 67-5-33, Contingent fee contracts (le.utah.gov)).

What happened next

  • On Oct. 20, AG Brown declared online gambling a public nuisance, finding the kind of harm H.B. 591 requires, and sued Kalshi, two Susquehanna firms and others in Third District Court (No. 260908356). The lawsuit's nuisance count relies on 78B-6a-102(2) (KUTV, Utah Attorney General declares online gambling public nuisance in lawsuit against Kalshi, Oct. 20, 2026; Deseret News, Dennis Romboy, Utah sues Kalshi, Oct. 20, 2026; United States and CFTC v. State of Utah and Derek Brown, No. 2:26-cv-01055 (D. Utah), complaint filed Oct. 26, 2026). See what the AG's cases cite on the H.B. 243 page.
  • On Oct. 26, the United States and the CFTC sued Utah and Brown in federal court, saying federal law, not Utah's, governs these markets (United States and CFTC v. State of Utah and Derek Brown, No. 2:26-cv-01055 (D. Utah), complaint filed Oct. 26, 2026).
  • Criminal charges against Kalshi and its founders, dated Oct. 28, use the gambling laws, not the nuisance law (State of Utah v. KalshiEX, LLC, Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, Criminal Information and Declaration of Probable Cause, Third District Court, Salt Lake County, dated Oct. 28, 2026 (AGO No. 9465862), posted by Utah News Dispatch).
  • We found no other case in which the AG has used the exception; a news search on Nov. 4 turned up only the Kalshi suit. The AG does not have to report his uses until before Oct. 1, 2029 (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text; KUTV, Utah Attorney General declares online gambling public nuisance in lawsuit against Kalshi, Oct. 20, 2026).
  • The nuisance exception is not limited to gambling. The bill sets no list of what the AG may call a nuisance; it requires his finding of substantial ongoing harm to a significant number of Utahns (H.B. 591 enrolled (final) text).
