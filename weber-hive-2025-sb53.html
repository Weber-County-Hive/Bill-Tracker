The Residency Rule, Part Two

An election-code cleanup bill dropped the rule that people gathering signatures for party candidates must live in Utah, and gave signers of those petitions a way to take their names back.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • S.B. 53 (2025), primary sponsor Sen. Calvin R. Musselman and House sponsor Rep. Stephanie Gricius, is an "Election Code Amendments" bill with several unrelated changes (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text).
  • One change: people who gather signatures for party candidates, and for new political parties, no longer have to live in Utah (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text). S.B. 107 (2024) had already dropped that rule for initiatives, referendums and independent candidates (Bill-Tracker: S.B. 107 (2024), The Residency Rule That Went Away).
  • Another change: a voter who signs a party candidate's petition can now ask to have the signature removed (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text).
  • Sen. Musselman told a House committee the state expected to lose if sued over the residency rule. He cited a 2008 Oklahoma case, KUER reported (KUER, Jan. 30, 2025).
  • It passed the Senate 25–1 and the House 67–0, and took effect May 7, 2025 (LegiScan, S.B. 53 (2025) history; S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text). Bracketed words are struck.

Highlighted provisions (lines 16–17)

repeals the in-state residency requirement for a signature-gatherer who circulates a petition on behalf of:

The list that follows names new political parties and candidates seeking a party's nomination (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-9-405(7) · lines 864–867

(7) A nomination petition circulator[ must be at least 18 years old and a resident of the state, but may affiliate with any political party.] :

The new text reads "(a) must be at least 18 years old; and (b) may affiliate with any political party." (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text; Utah Code 20A-9-405, 2025 copy (Justia), amended by Chapter 38, 2025)

Utah Code 20A-9-408(9)(d)(i) · lines 1013–1016

A registered voter who physically signs a form under Subsections (8) and (9)(b) may have the voter's signature removed from the form

The voter has until three business days after the form is turned in to ask (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text).

Effective date · line 1148

This bill takes effect on May 7, 2025.

(S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text)

The timeline

  1. Feb. 28, 2024 S.B. 107 (2024) is signed, dropping residency for initiative, referendum and independent-candidate gatherers. Party-candidate gatherers keep the rule (Bill-Tracker: S.B. 107 (2024), The Residency Rule That Went Away).
  2. 2024 interim The Government Operations Interim Committee recommends the bill 10–0 (S.B. 53 (2025) as introduced).
  3. Jan. 2, 2025 S.B. 53 is numbered and made public (LegiScan, S.B. 53 (2025) history).
  4. Jan. 21, 2025 The Senate passes it 25–1–3 (LegiScan, S.B. 53 (2025) history; LegiScan, Senate roll call, 2nd & 3rd readings).
  5. Jan. 30, 2025 House committee recommends it 11–0. Sen. Musselman says the state expected to lose a lawsuit over the residency rule (House Government Operations Committee vote, Jan. 30, 2025; KUER, Jan. 30, 2025).
  6. Feb. 5, 2025 The House passes it 67–0–8 (LegiScan, S.B. 53 (2025) history; LegiScan, House roll call, 3rd reading).

  8. May 6, 2026 H.B. 32 (2026) takes effect, requiring state training for every signature gatherer (Bill-Tracker: H.B. 32 (2026), Training for Every Signature Gatherer).

What happened next

  • KUER reported the residency repeal was tied to a 2008 Oklahoma case in which "the court ruled the requirement violated citizens' First Amendment rights" (KUER, Jan. 30, 2025).
  • The Utah Code now says a nomination petition circulator "must be at least 18 years old" and "may affiliate with any political party," with no residency rule (Utah Code 20A-9-405, 2025 copy (Justia), amended by Chapter 38, 2025).
  • Since May 6, 2026, every gatherer, including those for candidates, must finish a state training course first (H.B. 32) (Bill-Tracker: H.B. 32 (2026), Training for Every Signature Gatherer).
