The Residency Rule That Went Away

A bill about independent candidates' deadlines picked up a repeal of the rule that petition signature gatherers live in Utah.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. S.B. 107 (2024) is one of them.

Short version

  • S.B. 107 (2024) began as a bill about deadlines for independent candidates ("Unaffiliated Candidate Amendments") (S.B. 107 as introduced).
  • In the House committee on Feb. 2, 2024, a 2nd Substitute added a repeal of the rule that people who collect signatures for initiatives, referendums and independent candidates must live in Utah (S.B. 107 2nd Substitute; House committee report, Feb. 2, 2024). The enrolled bill strikes residency language in 19 places in 11 code sections (S.B. 107 enrolled text).
  • It passed with wide margins after a conference committee and was signed Feb. 28, 2024 (S.B. 107 (2024) bill status page).
  • We found no written reason for the residency repeal. KSL reported the House sponsor, Rep. Jordan Teuscher, said helping the state's signature app "wasn't the purpose" (KSL, Feb. 19, 2024).
  • Party candidates' gatherers kept the residency rule until S.B. 53 (2025) dropped it (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text (Musselman / Gricius)). Since then, about half of the paid gatherers for the 2025–26 Prop 4 repeal initiative were from out of state, the Utah GOP chairman said, and a North Carolina gatherer pleaded no contest to forgery in Uintah County (Salt Lake Tribune, Dec. 8, 2025; KSL, June 9, 2026 (Uintah County plea)).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (S.B. 107 enrolled text). Bracketed words are struck.

Highlighted provisions (enrolled)

repeals the in-state residency requirement for individuals who collect petition signatures; repeals provisions related to the in-state residency requirement described above;

(S.B. 107 enrolled text)

Utah Code 20A-7-105(4)(a)(i) · lines 66–67

(i) is at least 18 years old [and meets the residency requirements of Section 20A-2-105]

One of 19 strikes (S.B. 107 enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-203(4), signature gatherer verification · line 305

I [am a resident of Utah and] am at least 18 years old;

The same strike appears in the statewide referendum, local initiative, local referendum, candidate and electronic signature forms (S.B. 107 enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-9-502(5)(a) · lines 855–858

To qualify for placement on the general election ballot, the candidate shall, no earlier than the start of the declaration of candidacy period described in Section 20A-9-201.5 and no later than 5 p.m. on June 15 of the year in which the election will be held:

The independent-candidate deadline change (S.B. 107 enrolled text).

The timeline

  1. June 2023 Charging documents in the 2nd District case later allege Nevada residents gathered party-candidate signatures while Utah required gatherers to live here (KSL, April 5, 2024 (13 charged in 2nd District signature case)). That rule for party-candidate petitions was not changed by S.B. 107.
  2. Dec. 7, 2023 After Robert F. Kennedy Jr. sues over the independent-candidate deadline, the Lt. Governor's office says "Key legislators are aware of the potential problems with the current statute" (KSL, Dec. 7, 2023 (Lt. Governor and the Kennedy lawsuit)).
  3. Jan. 16, 2024 S.B. 107 introduced, about the deadline only (S.B. 107 as introduced).
  4. Feb. 2, 2024 House committee adds the residency repeal (House committee report, Feb. 2, 2024).
  5. Feb. 22, 2024 Final passage after a conference committee (Senate final roll call, Feb. 22, 2024).
  6. Feb. 28, 2024 Gov. Cox signs it (S.B. 107 (2024) bill status page).
  7. April 5, 2024 13 people charged in the 2nd District signature case (KSL, April 5, 2024 (13 charged in 2nd District signature case)).
  8. Dec. 8, 2025 Utah GOP chairman Rob Axson: "About half of the signature gatherers being paid are from outside the state" for the Prop 4 repeal (Salt Lake Tribune, Dec. 8, 2025).
  9. May 7, 2025 S.B. 53 (Sen. Calvin Musselman, Rep. Stephanie Gricius) takes effect, dropping the residency rule for party candidates' gatherers too (S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text (Musselman / Gricius); Utah Code 20A-9-405, current copy (Justia), amended by Chapter 38, 2025).
  10. May 24, 2026 A North Carolina man pleads no contest to two forgery counts over Prop 4 repeal signatures in Uintah County; held in abeyance (KSL, June 9, 2026 (Uintah County plea)).

What happened next

  • The state's candidate guidance shows the second step. The 2024 Candidate Manual, which covers candidates' petitions, still said "Circulators must be a resident of Utah and at least 18 years old" (p. 18), even as it cited S.B. 107 for the new independent-candidate deadline (2024 Candidate Manual, Lt. Governor (Carbon County copy)). After S.B. 53 (2025), the 2026 manual says "Circulators must be at least 18 years old" (2026 Candidate Manual, Lt. Governor; S.B. 53 (2025) enrolled text (Musselman / Gricius)).
  • For the 2025–26 Prop 4 repeal initiative, the recruiting firm Patriot Grassroots is described as registered in Wyoming (Salt Lake Tribune, Dec. 8, 2025).
  • Chadwick Kluttz, of North Carolina, pleaded no contest on May 24, 2026 to two third-degree felony forgery counts over Prop 4 repeal signatures in Uintah County; the pleas are held in abeyance for two years and 20 other charges were dropped (KSL, June 9, 2026 (Uintah County plea)).
