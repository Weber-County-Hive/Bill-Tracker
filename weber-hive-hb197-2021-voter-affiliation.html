CASE 2021-197

H.B. 197 · 2021 General Session · Voter Affiliation Amendments

The Draft He Changed

H.B. 197 set deadlines for switching political parties before a primary election. Its sponsor, Rep. Jordan Teuscher, brought a third version to the House floor that dropped two limits his own earlier drafts had included, and added a narrower new one. The House adopted that version by voice vote, so no names were recorded. Twenty minutes later, it passed 41–30.

Primary Sponsor: Rep. Jordan D. Teuscher · Senate Sponsor: Sen. Curtis S. Bramble Effective: Became law without the governor's signature, March 24, 2021 Became Law

Posted Nov. 26, 2026 · Updated Nov. 26, 2026

New Here? Start Simple

"Party raiding" means joining another party just to vote in its primary.

In Utah, the Republican primary often decides who wins. Before 2021, voters could switch parties up to the sign-up deadline. Voters with no party could join one at the polls on primary day.

H.B. 197 (2021) set a cutoff date. In an election year, a switch from one party to another after the cutoff waits until after the primary.

The bill had three versions. The first two also made voters with no party wait. They also closed a path where a voter could drop off the voter list and sign up again to get around the cutoff.

The third version became law. It dropped both of those. It added a smaller rule for voters who left a party after April 1.

His campaign website says the bill "improves the integrity of Utah's primary election system by preventing party raiding." This page shows what each version said and how the votes were taken. Later laws may have changed these rules. This page covers the 2021 bill.

The Floor Votes

On Feb. 12, 2021, the bill came up on the House floor at 10:54 a.m. One minute later, at 10:55 a.m., the House replaced the committee version (S02) with S03 by voice vote. A voice vote records only that the motion passed. It does not record who voted which way.

At 11:15 a.m. the House passed S03 on a roll call, 41–30–4. This was not a near-unanimous bill: the House committee had passed it 7–3–1, and 30 House members voted no.

In the Senate, the bill passed committee 4–2–2, second reading 19–6–4 and third reading 22–3–4. The governor did not sign it; it became law without his signature on March 24, 2021. The record does not say why.

The Timeline

  • Jan. 19, 2021 — H.B. 197 introduced, with a Jan. 1 cutoff covering party switches and unaffiliated voters joining a party.
  • Feb. 5, 2021 — House Government Operations Committee swaps in the 2nd Substitute (10–0–1), then recommends it (7–3–1). The 1st Substitute was not adopted.
  • Feb. 12, 2021, 10:55 a.m. — On the House floor, S03 replaces S02 by voice vote.
  • Feb. 12, 2021, 11:15 a.m. — House passes S03, 41–30–4.
  • Feb. 22, 2021 — Senate committee recommends it, 4–2–2.
  • Feb. 25, 2021 — Senate second reading, 19–6–4 (after being circled and uncircled by voice vote).
  • March 3, 2021 — Senate final passage, 22–3–4.
  • March 24, 2021 — Becomes law without the governor's signature.

How This Could Have Been Clearer

  • A roll-call vote on adopting the floor substitute, so members are on record for what it removed
  • A short floor explanation, in the record, of why the two limits were dropped
  • The bill's description on the sponsor's site noting that the final version covers party-to-party switches, not unaffiliated voters joining a party

Three Ways to Look at It

An auditor's look: The paper trail is full. There are three versions, a side-by-side document, timed vote records and a roll call. The one gap is the 10:55 a.m. voice vote. It does not show who voted to drop the two limits.

The Legislature's look: House rules let members adopt a new version by voice vote. Sponsors often change bills on the floor. Any member could have asked for a roll call. The record shows none was taken.

The collective look: This touches every voter who switches or joins a party. The final law covers party-to-party switches. It does not cover voters with no party who join one near a primary. The sponsor's own earlier drafts did.

What could have been done differently: a roll call on the new version, and a short floor statement of why the two limits were dropped.

Questions to Ask, and Who to Ask

  1. Why were the unaffiliated-voter rule and the re-registration rule dropped between S02 and S03? The floor debate audio for Feb. 12, 2021 is posted on the bill page at le.utah.gov; listen there, or ask a member of the House Government Operations Committee.
  2. How many voters joined a party at the polls on primary day in 2022, 2024 and 2026? Ask your county clerk, or the Lieutenant Governor's Elections Office.
  3. Should a floor substitute that removes provisions require a roll-call vote? Ask your own state representative; House rules are set by the House.

Words to Know

  • Party raiding: When voters join another party just long enough to vote in its primary.
  • Unaffiliated: A registered voter who has not joined any political party.
  • Substitute: A full replacement version of a bill, numbered S01, S02, S03 and so on.
  • Voice vote: A vote where members call out yes or no together. It passes or fails, but no names are recorded.
  • Roll call: A vote where each member's yes or no is recorded by name.
