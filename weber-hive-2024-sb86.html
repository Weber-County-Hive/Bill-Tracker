A Cap and a Hearing for Bonds Voters Never See

S.B. 86 put a $200 million, three-year cap on local lease revenue bonds and required notice and a public evening hearing before big ones are issued. Voters still do not vote on them.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • S.B. 86 (2024), "Local Government Bonds Amendments," primary sponsor Sen. Lincoln Fillmore, House sponsor Rep. Brady Brammer, caps the lease revenue bonds a local political subdivision can issue at $200 million in any three years in a row (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in); LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls).
  • Before issuing a lease revenue bond over $10 million, a local government must make a statement at a meeting, post notice for at least 14 days, and hold a public hearing that starts at or after 6 p.m. (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).
  • A school district's notice must say the bond commits future property tax and income tax money, and that a lease revenue bond generally costs more in interest than a voter-approved general obligation bond (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).
  • The bill does not give voters a vote on lease revenue bonds (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).
  • It passed the Senate 19–5, the House 43–23, and the Senate again 20–6, and was signed March 20, 2024 (LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls).
  • In 2025, S.B. 188 wrote an exemption from this same cap for certain bonds in districts that are splitting (S.B. 188 (2025) enrolled text; The Weber County Hive, Bait and Switch Part 3, Case 11 (S.B. 188 and H.B. 332)).

What the bill says

Quoted from the 3rd Substitute, the version the House passed and the Senate concurred in (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in); LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls). The enrolled PDF on le.utah.gov is a scanned image that our tools could not read as text (S.B. 86 (2024) enrolled text (image-only PDF; could not be read as text)), so these quotes were not checked word for word against the enrolled copy.

Utah Code 11-14-103(6)(a)(ii)

the total of all lease revenue bonds issued by a local political subdivision within any consecutive three-year period.

This is how the bill defines the "combined total" (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).

Utah Code 11-14-103(6)(b)(i)

may not issue a lease revenue bond if the issuance of the bond will cause the combined total to exceed $200,000,000.

Bonds for a correctional facility do not count toward the total (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).

Utah Code 11-14-103(6)(c)

Before issuing an applicable lease revenue bond on or after May 1, 2024, a local political subdivision shall:

An "applicable" bond is one over $10 million (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).

Utah Code 11-14-103(6)(f)(ii)(A)

shall be held beginning at or after 6:00 p.m.;

The public hearing on the bond (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).

Utah Code 11-14-103(6)(e)(iii), school district notice

Additionally, a lease revenue bond generally has a higher interest cost than a voter-approved general obligation bond.

The same notice must say the bond "commits money from future property tax and income tax revenue allocated to the school district" (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).

Utah Code 17D-2-501(2)

shall comply with the same requirements and is subject to the same limitations under Subsection 11-14-103(6)

Applies the same rules to a local building authority issuing a lease revenue bond on or after May 1, 2024 (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. Brady Brammer (the version the Senate concurred in)).

  2. Jan. 10, 2024 S.B. 86 numbered (LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls).
  3. Feb. 7–14, 2024 Senate committee and Senate floor pass it (LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls).
  4. Feb. 29, 2024 House adopts Rep. Brammer's 3rd Substitute and passes it 43–23; the Senate concurs 20–6 the same day (LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls).
  5. March 5–7, 2024 Alpine School District gives notice that it intends to issue $175 million in lease revenue bonds on March 19; the Utah Taxpayers Association reports the total with earlier bonds would exceed the new cap (Utah Taxpayers Association, "Alpine School District Plans to Ignore Voters and Defy Legislature," Malah Armstrong, March 7, 2024).
  6. March 18, 2024 Alpine cancels the hearing on the $175 million bond (Daily Herald, "Alpine School District cancels public meeting on $175M bond for new high school," Carlene Coombs, March 18, 2024; Salt Lake Tribune, "Facing criticism, Alpine School District may back away from $175M lease revenue bond," Carmen Nesbitt, March 18, 2024).
  7. March 20, 2024 Gov. Spencer Cox signs S.B. 86 (LegiScan, S.B. 86 (2024): history and roll calls).
  8. May 1, 2024 The cap and hearing rules apply to bonds issued from this date (S.B. 86 (2024) 3rd Substitute, proposed by Rep. What happened next

  • Alpine School District canceled its March 19, 2024 hearing on a $175 million lease revenue bond. The Daily Herald reported the district had scheduled it partly because of the new law, and that the board later decided it had more time to act before May 1 (Daily Herald, "Alpine School District cancels public meeting on $175M bond for new high school," Carlene Coombs, March 18, 2024).
  • In 2025, S.B. 188 (primary sponsor Sen. Keith Grover) added that a lease revenue bond issued for a new district, after voters in the dividing district rejected a general obligation bond, "is not subject to the combined total limitation described in Subsection 11-14-103(6)(b)(i)" (S.B. 188 (2025) enrolled text; The Weber County Hive, Bait and Switch Part 3, Case 11 (S.B. 188 and H.B. 332)).
  • In 2026, H.B. 332 (Rep. Tiara Auxier) would have required a school district to put a general obligation bond to voters before issuing a lease revenue bond. It was held in committee and closed out on March 6, 2026 without a floor vote (The Weber County Hive, Bait and Switch Part 3, Case 11 (S.B. 188 and H.B. 332)).
  • We found no statement from the sponsors in the record we read explaining the bill.

Bills that change the same rule

  1. 2024 S.B. 86
    Created the $200 million, three-year cap on lease revenue bonds and required notice and an evening public hearing for bonds over $10 million.
  2. 2025 S.B. 188
    Exempted certain lease revenue bonds for new school districts from that cap when voters in the dividing district rejected a general obligation bond.
  3. 2026 H.B. 332
    Would have required a public vote on a general obligation bond before any school district lease revenue bond. Held in committee; died March 6, 2026.
