The Prop-Bet Bill the AG's Office Asked to Widen

H.B. 243 made proposition bets gambling in Utah. In committee, the sponsor said the Attorney General's office asked to take out the words that limited it to sports. That October, the AG used Utah's gambling laws to sue and charge Kalshi.

Short version

  • H.B. 243 (2026), "Gambling Revisions," adds "proposition bets" to Utah's legal definition of gambling. A proposition bet is "a gambling bet on an individual action, statistic, occurrence, or non-occurrence" (H.B. 243 enrolled (final) text).
  • As introduced, the bill reached only bets placed during an athletic event. On Jan. 30, 2026, the House committee voted 8–0–2 to take that limit out (House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2026; House committee vote, Jan. 30, 2026 (8-0-2)). In the hearing, sponsor Rep. Joseph Elison said "the Attorney General's office asked for this amendment," and the AG's chief deputy said taking out "athletic" reaches bets on the Fed, the Oscars and the Grammys (House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee video, Jan. 30, 2026 (H.B. 243 at about 0:36-0:45)).
  • Attorney General Derek Brown presented the bill himself in the Senate committee on Feb. 19 (Senate Business and Labor Committee minutes, Feb. 19, 2026; Senate Business and Labor Committee video, Feb. 19, 2026 (H.B. 243 at about 1:03-1:12)). It passed the House 63–9–3 and the Senate 25–0–4. Gov. Cox signed it March 25, and it took effect May 6, 2026 (H.B. 243 (2026) Gambling Revisions, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; House 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 10, 2026 (63-9-3); Senate 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 27, 2026 (25-0-4)).
  • The same day, May 6, H.B. 591 took effect. It ended most common-law nuisance lawsuits but kept one for the AG when he finds "substantial ongoing harm to a significant number of the citizens of Utah" (H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, le.utah.gov (Utah Code 78B-6a-102)). Sen. Brady Brammer sponsored both bills in the Senate (H.B. 243 (2026) Gambling Revisions, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, le.utah.gov (Utah Code 78B-6a-102)).
  • Derek Brown's campaign gave Brammer $2,000 on June 3, 2026, and gave H.B. 591's House sponsor, Rep. New to this? Betting apps, explained simply

Most of us have never used these apps. Here is what each kind is, in everyday words.

A regular betYou and a friend each put in $5 on who wins the big game. Whoever picks the winner gets the $10. Utah's Constitution has never allowed this kind of betting.
A prop bet ("proposition bet")Instead of betting on who wins, you bet on one small thing: "Will the quarterback throw more than 2 touchdowns?" or "Will the coach say a certain word in his interview?" It is still a bet. You win money or lose it. H.B. 243 says these count as gambling in Utah.
A prediction market (like Kalshi)An app sells "yes" and "no" tickets on almost anything: a game, an election, whether the Fed changes interest rates. If a "yes" ticket costs 60 cents and the answer turns out to be yes, it pays $1; if not, you lose your 60 cents. The companies call this "trading," like the stock market, and say federal regulators (the CFTC), not states, are in charge of them. Utah's AG calls it gambling: "a distinction without a difference."
A sweepstakes casino (like VGW's Chumba)An online slot-machine style site that uses two kinds of play money. One kind can be traded for real prizes. The companies say it is not gambling because "no purchase is necessary." People in Utah have sued some of them, and those suits are not decided. H.B. 243 does not deal with these.
Fantasy sports and "pick'em" apps (like PrizePicks, Underdog)You pick players and win if they do well. Some states call it skill, some call it gambling. H.B. 243's sponsor said the bill does not deal with fantasy leagues. Some of these companies now also sell prediction-market tickets.
Why this fight mattersIf federal law covers these apps, Utah may not be able to stop them, even though its Constitution bans gambling. That question is now in federal court, and Utah's case is part of it.

What the bill says

Quoted from the final, enrolled bill (H.B. 243 enrolled (final) text).

Utah Code 76-9-1401, "Gambling" includes

(i) a lottery; and (ii) a proposition bet.

Utah Code 76-9-1401, new definition

"Proposition bet" means a gambling bet on an individual action, statistic, occurrence, or non-occurrence.

That is the whole change. The bill reprints the rest of the definitions section, but the rules about fringe gaming devices, sweepstakes and promotions were already law; they came from earlier bills (see Where it fits) (H.B. 243 enrolled (final) text).

What it does not do: it adds no new crime or penalty, and it does not address sweepstakes casinos or fantasy sports. Rep. Elison said on the House floor that fantasy leagues are "not" addressed by the bill (House floor video, Feb. 10, 2026 (H.B. 243 at about 0:33-0:42)). The penalties for gambling already sit elsewhere in Utah Code Title 76, Chapter 9, Part 14.

Utah's Constitution already says the Legislature "shall not authorize any game of chance, lottery or gift enterprise" (Utah Constitution, Article VI, Section 27). On the House floor, Rep. Elison said the AG's office told him that without this bill, a prop bet "most likely in a court of law, it would still be considered, um, not gambling" (House floor video, Feb. 10, 2026 (H.B. 243 at about 0:33-0:42)).

Every version, and what changed

VersionDateWhat changed
IntroducedJan. 13, 2026Added "proposition bet" to the gambling definition, limited to bets during an athletic event (H.B. 243 as introduced).
House committee Amendment #1 (moved by Rep. Paul Cutler)Jan. 30, 2026Took out the athletic-event limit, so the definition covers any "individual action, statistic, occurrence, or non-occurrence." Adopted 8–0–2 (House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee minutes, Jan. 30, 2026; House committee vote, Jan. 30, 2026 (8-0-2); H.B. 243 as amended in House committee (Feb. 2, 2026)).
Enrolled (final)March 2026Same as the amended bill. No further changes in the Senate (H.B. 243 enrolled (final) text; H.B. 243 (2026) Gambling Revisions, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
The change that matters. With the word "athletic" in it, the bill covered sports prop bets. Without it, the bill covers bets on anything, which is how prediction-market apps work. The AG's chief deputy said so in the hearing: "by taking out the word athletic, it, it broadens it to anything else that we would find in those apps" (House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee video, Jan. 30, 2026 (H.B. 243 at about 0:36-0:45)).

What happened next

  • Kalshi sued Utah in federal court in February. A federal judge ruled for Utah in August, and the 10th Circuit declined to pause that ruling on Sept. 8 (The Ledger, Case 17: Casino Money and the Attorney General (Weber County Hive)).
  • On Oct. 20, the AG sued Kalshi in state court and declared gambling a public nuisance (KUTV, Utah Attorney General declares online gambling public nuisance in lawsuit against Kalshi, Oct. 20, 2026; Deseret News, Dennis Romboy, Utah sues Kalshi, Oct. 20, 2026; United States and CFTC v. See what the AG's cases cite.
  • On Oct. 26, the United States and the CFTC sued Utah and Brown in federal court, saying federal law covers these markets (United States and CFTC v. State of Utah and Derek Brown, No. 2:26-cv-01055 (D. Utah), complaint filed Oct. 26, 2026).
  • The 2027 session is the next chance for sweepstakes or prediction-market bills. VGW's gifts went to 21 legislators between Aug. 31 and Oct. 29, 2026 (The Ledger, Case 17: Casino Money and the Attorney General (Weber County Hive)).

Where it fits: Utah vs. federal authority

Utah lawmakers often pass laws to push back on federal control. This case is that same fight, from a new direction.

The usual Utah patternThis case
Utah says federal agencies reach too far, and passes laws to claim state control, for example over public lands and federal money. The Legislature's Federalism Commission recommends many of these bills; see S.B. 44 (2026), H.B. 249 (2026) and H.B. 546 (2026) on the Bill-Tracker (Utah Legislature, Federalism Commission meeting record (le.utah.gov))Kalshi and the federal government say federal law controls these betting markets, so Utah cannot stop them. Utah says its Constitution and its own gambling laws come first (United States and CFTC v. State of Utah and Derek Brown, No. 2:26-cv-01055 (D. Utah), complaint filed Oct. 26, 2026; Utah Constitution, Article VI, Section 27)
Utah builds its case with new state laws, then goes to court.Utah widened its gambling definition (H.B. 243), kept a nuisance lawsuit only the AG can bring (H.B. 591), then sued and charged Kalshi using both kinds of law (H.B. 243 enrolled (final) text; H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, le.utah.gov (Utah Code 78B-6a-102); United States and CFTC v. State of Utah and Derek Brown, No. 2:26-cv-01055 (D. Utah), complaint filed Oct. 26, 2026; State of Utah v. KalshiEX, LLC, Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, Criminal Information and Declaration of Probable Cause, Third District Court, Salt Lake County, dated Oct. 28, 2026 (AGO No. 9465862), posted by Utah News Dispatch)
how they're trying to set themselves up as being regulated by the feds and not the states. And we've been working to push back on those efforts.

— Daniel Burton, Office of the Attorney General, House committee, Jan. 30, 2026, nine months before the Kalshi lawsuit (House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee video, Jan. 30, 2026 (H.B. 243 at about 0:36-0:45))

AG Derek Brown is also listed as a speaker at the Legislature's first National Federalism Summit in September 2025, titled "Rebalancing the State-Federal Relationship" (UVU Center for Constitutional Studies, Inaugural National Federalism Summit program, 'Rebalancing the State-Federal Relationship,' Sept. 2025). The Kalshi fight now sits in the same place as Utah's public-lands and federal-funds fights: a federal court deciding where federal power ends and state power begins (United States and CFTC v. State of Utah and Derek Brown, No. 2:26-cv-01055 (D. Utah), complaint filed Oct. 26, 2026).
