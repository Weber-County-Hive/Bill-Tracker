The Zoning Override That Became a Study

As introduced, H.B. 502 would have required every city and county to allow mining by the end of 2024. What passed was a $500,000 state study.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • As introduced, H.B. 502 (2024), primary sponsor Rep. Casey Snider, said every city and county must allow mining and critical infrastructure materials operations as a permitted or conditional use by Dec. 31, 2024 (H.B. 502 as introduced).
  • The House committee replaced it on Feb. 22, 2024 with a 1st Substitute: a study by the Division of Oil, Gas, and Mining, with no zoning mandate (1st Substitute H.B. 502; Compare: 1st Substitute vs. introduced; House Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment minutes, Feb. 22, 2024).
  • On the Senate floor, a 2nd Substitute added a $500,000 one-time appropriation, a final report and a July 1, 2026 repeal date (H.B. 502 (2024) bill status page, le.utah.gov; Compare: 2nd Substitute vs. 1st Substitute).
  • It passed every vote but drew double-digit no votes at each floor stage. Gov. Cox signed it March 12, 2024 (H.B. 502 (2024) bill status page, le.utah.gov).

What the bill says

Quoted from the introduced bill (H.B. 502 as introduced) and the enrolled bill (H.B. 502 enrolled text).

Proposed Utah Code 17-41-402.2(1)(a), introduced

shall allow critical infrastructure materials operations and mining operations as a permitted or conditional use

Deadline: Dec. 31, 2024 (H.B. 502 as introduced).

Proposed Utah Code 17-41-402.2(1)(b), introduced

is considered to allow critical infrastructure materials operations and mining operations as a permitted use

Applied to a city or county that missed the deadline (H.B. 502 as introduced).

Utah Code 17-41-102(2), enrolled

The division shall conduct a study of critical infrastructure materials operations and related mining.

(H.B. 502 enrolled text)

The timeline

  1. Feb. 7, 2024 H.B. 502 numbered and made public (H.B. 502 (2024) bill status page, le.utah.gov).
  2. Feb. 22, 2024 House committee hears seven witnesses, then adopts the study-only 1st Substitute (House Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment minutes, Feb. 22, 2024).
  3. Feb. 26, 2024 House passes it 57–16–2 (H.B. 502 (2024) bill status page, le.utah.gov).
  4. Feb. 27, 2024 Senate committee hears nine witnesses, two in opposition, and recommends it 5–2–1 (Senate Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment draft minutes (H.B. 502)).
  5. March 1, 2024 Senate adopts the 2nd Substitute and passes it 19–9–1; House concurs 54–17–4 (H.B. 502 (2024) bill status page, le.utah.gov).
  6. March 12, 2024 Gov. Cox signs it (H.B. 502 (2024) bill status page, le.utah.gov).
  7. July 1, 2026 The study section's repeal date (H.B. 502 enrolled text).

What happened next

  • The study was due to the Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment Interim Committee by Nov. 1, 2024, with a final report by the start of the 2025 session (H.B. 502 enrolled text).
  • The study section repeals July 1, 2026 (H.B. 502 enrolled text).
  • Two years later, S.B. 254 (2026) set up critical minerals zones; see the Bill-Tracker case file (Bill Tracker: S.B. 254 (2026)).
