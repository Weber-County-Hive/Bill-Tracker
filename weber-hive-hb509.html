CASE 2026-509

H.B. 509 · 2026 General Session

From Protection to Just a Study

Introduced as “Wetlands Protection Modifications,” this bill was narrowed to a study only — scope cut from statewide to the state's largest counties, its acreage baseline and species-impact provisions struck, and a required study on a real wetland-mitigation funding mechanism removed entirely before passage.

Chief Sponsor: Rep. Doug OwensSenate Sponsor: Sen. Michael K. McKell Effective: May 6, 2026 Became Law

New Here? Start Simple

Utah is losing wetlands — the marshy areas that filter water and support wildlife around places like the Great Salt Lake.

This bill started out studying that loss statewide, tracking how much wetland acreage had actually disappeared over five years, and requiring a real study on creating a funding program to make up for wetlands lost to development.

By the time it passed, the study only covers the state's most populous counties, the acreage-change tracking is gone, and the funding-program study was removed entirely.

What Changed, Section by Section

As Introduced (&ldquo;Wetlands Protection Modifications&rdquo;)

Metrics5-year wetland-acreage-change baseline; impacts on recreation and aquatic species
Funding mechanismRequired a separate study on an in-lieu fee program for wetland mitigation, including consultation with DEQ, the Army Corps of Engineers, and Fish and Wildlife Service
As Enacted (&ldquo;Wetlands Study Amendments&rdquo;)

MetricsAcreage-change baseline and species/recreation provisions struck

How This Could Have Actually Protected Wetlands

A version of this bill that kept its original purpose would have:

  • Kept the statewide scope, since wetland loss isn't limited to the state's most populous counties
  • Kept the five-year acreage-change baseline — without it, there's no way to measure whether wetland loss is getting better or worse over time
  • Kept the required study on an in-lieu fee program, since that was the one piece of this bill that could have led to an actual funding mechanism for wetland mitigation, not just more research about it
