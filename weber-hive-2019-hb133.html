When a Voter-Passed Law Starts

H.B. 133 moved the start date of laws passed by statewide initiative to after the next legislative session, and moved conflict appeals out of the Supreme Court.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. H.B. 133 (2019) is one of them.

Short version

  • H.B. 133 (2019), primary sponsor Rep. Brad M. Daw, changed when a law passed by statewide initiative takes effect (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text; H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page).
  • Before, a voter-approved law took effect five days after the governor's proclamation of the vote, or on a date the initiative named (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text).
  • Now most take effect 60 days after the last day of the next general session of the Legislature. Tax changes have their own later or earlier dates (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text).
  • Voters who signed an initiative that the governor declares superseded now go to "the appropriate court," not the Utah Supreme Court (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text).
  • It passed the House 50–20–5 and the Senate 22–5–2, and was signed March 25, 2019 (House roll call, March 4, 2019; Senate roll call, March 13, 2019; H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text). Words in [brackets] are struck by the bill.

Highlighted Provisions, line 12

modifies the effective date of laws enacted by statewide initiative;

(H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text)

Utah Code 20A-7-212(2), lines 69-73

A proposed law submitted to the people by initiative petition that is approved by the voters at an election takes effect: (a) except as provided in Subsections (2)(b) through (e), on the day that is 60 days after the last day of the general session of the Legislature next following the election;

The struck rule said such a law "does not take effect until at least five days after the date of the official proclamation of the vote by the governor" (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-212(2)(b), lines 74-77

if the proposed law effectuates a tax increase: (i) except as provided in Subsection (2)(b)(ii), January 1 of the year after the general session of the Legislature next following the election;

Tax decreases take effect April 1 after the election, or at the start of the next tax year for income and franchise taxes (H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-211(3)(c), lines 42-45

any qualified voter who signed the initiative petition proposing the law that is declared by the governor to be superseded by another measure approved at the same election may [apply to the Supreme Court] bring an action in the appropriate court to review the governor's decision.

(H.B. 133 (2019) enrolled text)

The timeline

  1. Feb.–March 2018 H.B. 471 (Daw), to delay when voter-approved initiatives take effect, passes the House and dies in the Senate (H.B. 471 (2018) bill page; Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026 (Bill Tracker)).
  2. Nov. 2018 Voters pass Prop 2, the medical cannabis initiative (NORML, Dec. 6, 2018).
  3. Dec. 3, 2018 In a special session, the Legislature replaces Prop 2 with H.B. 3001 (NORML, Dec. 6, 2018).
  4. Jan. 16, 2019 H.B. 133 numbered and publicly distributed (H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page).
  5. Feb. 5, 2019 KPCW reports Daw's reasons, using Prop 2 as the example (KPCW, Feb. 5, 2019).
  6. Feb. 27, 2019 House committee adopts the 2nd Substitute and recommends it (H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page; House Government Operations, substitute vote, Feb. 27, 2019; House Government Operations, favorable vote, Feb. 27, 2019).
  7. March 4, 2019 House passes it 50–20–5 (House roll call, March 4, 2019).
  8. March 13, 2019 Senate passes it 22–5–2 (Senate roll call, March 13, 2019).
  9. March 25, 2019 Gov. Gary Herbert signs it (H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page).
  10. May 14, 2019 The new start-date rule takes effect (H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page).

What happened next

  • The rule took effect May 14, 2019 (H.B. 133 (2019) bill status page).
  • KUER reported this was the second year in a row Daw had brought the idea; the 2018 version, H.B. 471, died in the Senate (KUER, Feb. 27, 2019; H.B. 471 (2018) bill page).
  • The same 2019 session passed four other petition laws: H.B. 119, H.B. 145, H.B. 195 and S.B. 151 (Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026 (Bill Tracker)).
