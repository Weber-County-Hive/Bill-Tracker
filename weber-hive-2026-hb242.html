Rules for Taking Signatures Back

A bill about paid signature-removal gatherers picked up a last-night ban on removal requests mailed with prepaid postage, in the middle of the Prop 4 repeal fight.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. H.B. 242 (2026) is one of them.

Short version

  • H.B. 242 (2026), primary sponsor Rep. Karen Peterson, set rules for people who gather signature-removal statements: 18 or older; paid gatherers wear a badge, include a notice and are paid only by the hour (H.B. 242 (2026) enrolled text).
  • It also says a clerk "may not refuse to certify" a signature solely because its date is off by one calendar year (H.B. 242 (2026) enrolled text).
  • A ban on removal requests mailed with prepaid postage came in Senate floor substitutes. The final one, proposed by Sen. Brady Brammer and posted the night before, was adopted at about 10:56 p.m. on the session's last night; the bill passed minutes later (3rd Substitute (Sen. Brady Brammer, March 5); Utah News Dispatch, March 9, 2026).
  • Gov. Cox signed it the next morning, March 7, 2026, and it took effect that day (Utah News Dispatch, March 9, 2026; Utah Code 20A-7-104.5 (Chapter 27, effective March 7, 2026)).
  • At the time, Better Boundaries was running a removal drive against the Prop 4 repeal initiative. The repeal still fell short in April (Deseret News, Feb. 24, 2026 (lawsuit filed); KPCW, May 1, 2026).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (H.B. 242 (2026) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-1-1003(2)(b), lines 99–105

(b)(i) ... A voter may not submit a statement described in Subsection (1) by mail using prepaid postage, email, or other electronic means. (ii) Notwithstanding Subsection (2)(b)(i), a clerk may accept a statement described in Subsection (1) submitted by mail using prepaid postage if the statement is postmarked on or before the effective date of this bill.

(H.B. 242 (2026) enrolled text)

Utah Code 20A-7-104.5(2), lines 145–148

Beginning on May 6, 2026, an individual described in Subsection (1) who is paid to gather a statement shall, while gathering a statement, display a badge on the front of the individual's torso

The badge shows an ID number, the petition title, "Paid Signature Removal Statement Gatherer" and who pays them (lines 149–158) (H.B. 242 (2026) enrolled text).

Utah Code 20A-7-104.5(3), lines 159–160

A person may not pay an individual to gather a signature removal statement under this chapter unless the payment is based on an hourly rate.

(H.B. 242 (2026) enrolled text)

The timeline

  1. February 2026 The Prop 4 repeal initiative files its signatures, and Better Boundaries runs a removal drive (KPCW, March 3, 2026).
  2. Feb. 24, 2026 The repeal campaign's committee sues over prepaid removal letters (Deseret News, Feb. 24, 2026 (lawsuit filed)).
  3. Feb. 26, 2026 Senate committee recommends H.B. 242; the prepaid-postage exchange (Utah News Dispatch, March 9, 2026).
  4. Feb. 27 – March 5 Three Senate substitutes posted, the last by Sen. Brammer at 8:25 p.m. March 5 (1st Substitute (Sen. Todd Weiler, Feb. 27); 2nd Substitute (Sen. Brady Brammer, March 4); 3rd Substitute (Sen. Brady Brammer, March 5)).
  5. March 6, 2026, about 10:56 p.m. Senate adopts the 3rd Substitute; passes 21–7 four minutes later; House concurs 57–12 within 20 minutes (Utah News Dispatch, March 9, 2026).
  6. March 7, 2026 Gov. Cox signs it (Utah News Dispatch, March 9, 2026).
  7. March 13, 2026 The campaign's committee moves to dismiss its own lawsuit (Utah Political Watch, March 13, 2026 (lawsuit dropped)).
  8. April 30, 2026 The Lt. Governor finds the repeal petition insufficient: 161,961 valid, threshold met in 24 of 26 needed Senate districts (KPCW, May 1, 2026).

What happened next

  • The Lt. Governor's office told clerks to reject removal requests that arrive with prepaid postage, KSL reported (KSL/Deseret, March 12, 2026).
  • By March 26, removals had pushed one Senate district below the threshold, KUER reported (KUER, March 26, 2026). On April 30, the repeal petition was found insufficient (KPCW, May 1, 2026).
  • We found no published count of removal requests rejected under the prepaid-postage ban.

Where it fits

How this law builds on the ones before it, from How they stack.

Taking your signature back

  1. 2019 H.B. 145 C
    changed the form for removing a signature
  2. 2021 H.B. 211 C
    made state and local forms and steps the same, including removals
  3. 2023 H.B. 68 C
    put the rules for gathering and removing signatures in one place
  4. 2024 H.B. 79 B−
    added a way for people with a disability to sign or remove a signature
  5. 2026 H.B. 242 C+
    rules for people who gather removals: badges, a notice, hourly pay, no prepaid postage

Where it ended up: Removal rules grew from one form in 2019 to their own set of gatherer rules in 2026.
