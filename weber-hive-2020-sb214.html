The 2020 Law That Made Offering Online Gambling a Felony

In 2020, Utah banned sweepstakes gambling machines, raised gambling penalties, let cities and counties seize gambling money, and let people who lost money sue for double. Six years later, the felony this bill created is the one Utah charged against Kalshi.

The story in 5 steps

1. The problem in 2020Some machines gave players a chance at money or prizes but were called "sweepstakes," and their owners said that meant they weren't gambling. Kearns Metro Township's lawyer helped present a bill to stop them, and cities and police spoke for it.
2. What the law didIt called these "fringe gaming devices" and made profiting from them a crime. It raised the penalty for running gambling. It made offering online gambling to people in Utah a felony.
3. Tools for towns and peopleCities and counties can seize gambling money and machines. A person who loses money to one of these machines can sue the operator for double the loss.
4. How it passedCities, police, the PTA and others spoke for it. No one spoke against it in committee. One House member tried to strip the tougher penalties and the right to sue; that failed. It passed with more than two-thirds, so it took effect the day the governor signed it.
5. Why it matters nowIn October 2026, Utah charged Kalshi with 12 felony counts of offering online gambling. That felony level comes from this bill. In 2026, H.B. 591 went the other way for nuisance cases: it narrowed who can sue, and limited when cities can.

Every step is backed by the records further down this page (S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text; Senate Business and Labor Committee minutes, Mar. 3, 2020; House motion to substitute (2nd Sub.), failed on voice vote, Mar. 11, 2020; State of Utah v. KalshiEX, LLC, Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, Criminal Information and Declaration of Probable Cause, Third District Court, Salt Lake County, dated Oct. 28, 2026 (AGO No. 9465862), posted by Utah News Dispatch; H.B. 591 (2026) Nuisance Amendments, Weber County Hive Bill-Tracker).

Where were the machines?

In simple words: these were not casinos. They were a few slot-style machines in the corner of a convenience store, pawn shop, laundromat or bar, sold as "sweepstakes."

Short version

  • S.B. 214 (2020), "Gambling Machine and Sweepstakes Amendments," defines a "fringe gaming device": a machine that is not an arcade game or vending machine and gives a chance at money or a prize through a "contest, game, gaming scheme, or sweepstakes." It includes any similar machine "that seeks to avoid application or circumvent" the law or the Utah Constitution (S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text).
  • Profiting from one is a Class A misdemeanor the first time and a 3rd-degree felony after that. Offering "any form of Internet or online gambling to any person in this state" went from a Class A misdemeanor to a 3rd-degree felony. Gambling promotion and having a gambling device went from Class B to Class A (S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text).
  • Cities and counties may seize "gambling debts, gambling proceeds, or fringe gaming devices." A person who "suffers economic loss" from these machines may sue the operator and "recover twice the amount" (S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text).
  • Sen. Karen Mayne sponsored it, with Rep. Timothy D. Hawkes in the House and Sen. Curtis S. Bramble as cosponsor. Kearns Metro Township's legal counsel helped present it (S.B. 214 (2020) Gambling Machine and Sweepstakes Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; Senate Business and Labor Committee minutes, Mar. 3, 2020).
  • It passed the Senate 26–1–2 and the House 63–8–4. Gov. Herbert signed it March 28, 2020, and it took effect that day (Senate 3rd reading roll call, Mar. 6, 2020 (26-1-2); House 3rd reading roll call, Mar. 11, 2020 (63-8-4); S.B. 214 (2020) Gambling Machine and Sweepstakes Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  • The October 2026 charges against Kalshi list 12 counts of online gambling promotion as 3rd-degree felonies. This is the bill that made that a felony. The law has since been moved to Title 76, Chapter 9, Part 14 (State of Utah v. KalshiEX, LLC, Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, Criminal Information and Declaration of Probable Cause, Third District Court, Salt Lake County, dated Oct. 28, 2026 (AGO No. 9465862), posted by Utah News Dispatch; S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text).

What the bill says

In simple words: the chart shows which crimes got tougher. The quotes are from the final bill (S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text).

Online gambling (then 76-10-1102(3))

A person is guilty of a [class A misdemeanor] third degree felony who intentionally provides or offers to provide any form of Internet or online gambling to any person in this state.

Brackets show the words taken out.

Local control (then 76-10-1112)

a county or municipality may seize gambling debts, gambling proceeds, or fringe gaming devices that are reasonably identifiable as being obtained or provided in violation of this part or a local ordinance.

The right to sue (then 76-10-1113)

An individual who suffers economic loss as a result of a fringe gaming device, video gaming device, or gambling device or record may bring a cause of action against a person who operates or receives revenue from [it] to recover damages, costs, and attorney fees.

The person "may recover twice the amount of the economic loss."

It also defines "amusement device," "appropriate reward," "sweepstakes," "skill-based game" and "vending machine," and lists features that count against a machine, such as a "pre-reveal" of the result or a display that looks like a casino game (S.B. 214 (2020) enrolled (final) text). These definitions are what H.B. 243 reprinted in 2026.

Every version, and what changed

In simple words: the Senate softened the bill for people who only play, kept it tough for people who run gambling, and the House turned down an attempt to weaken it.

VersionWhat changed
Introduced (Feb. 28)Would have made playing a Class A misdemeanor and a repeat a felony (S.B. 214 (2020) as introduced)
1st Substitute (Senate committee, Mar. 3)Kept playing at Class B (repeat Class A). Kept the felony for offering online gambling, the seizure power and the double-damages lawsuit. How it connects to 2026

In simple words: this 2020 law is part of the toolbox Utah used in 2026. And in two places, the 2026 nuisance law went the opposite direction.

Money and lobbyists

The state's campaign files from 2018 on show no gambling-industry gifts to Sen. Mayne, Rep. Hawkes or Sen. Bramble. The first gambling-industry gift in those files went to then-AG Sean Reyes in October 2018; gambling money to Utah candidates stayed small until 2025 (The Ledger, Case 17: Casino Money and the Attorney General (Weber County Hive); disclosures.utah.gov, candidate and PAC filings and 'Download Data by Year' files, 2018-2026).
