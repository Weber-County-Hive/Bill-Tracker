"May, But Is Not Required To": The Redistricting Replacement

Voters passed an independent redistricting commission in 2018. S.B. 200 repealed it and created a new commission whose maps the Legislature does not have to vote on. A judge later ruled the repeal unconstitutional.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • Voters approved Proposition 4 in 2018, 50.3% to 49.7%. It created an independent commission to draw maps for the Legislature to approve or reject (Ballotpedia News, Aug. 29, 2025).
  • Under Prop 4, if the Legislature passed a different map, it had to publish a written report within seven days explaining why (Utah Code 20A-19-204 (2019), enacted by Proposition 4 (Justia)).
  • S.B. 200 (2020) repealed all nine sections of Prop 4, including that report and a section letting citizens sue (S.B. 200 enrolled text). It created a new seven-member commission whose maps the Legislature "may, but is not required to," vote on (S.B. 200 enrolled text).
  • Kept: a commission, a public meeting to present its maps, and anti-favoritism standards for the commission's own maps (S.B. 200 enrolled text). Removed: standards binding the Legislature, the report, and the right to sue (S.B. 200 enrolled text; Ballotpedia News, Aug. 29, 2025).
  • It had committee hearings in both chambers and passed 25–0 and 67–4 (S.B. 200 (2020) bill status page). In August 2025, a judge ruled the repeal of Prop 4 and S.B. 200 unconstitutional (KUER, Aug. 25, 2025, "Judge tosses out Utah's congressional maps").

What the bill says

Short quotes from the enrolled bill on le.utah.gov (S.B. 200 enrolled text) and from the Prop 4 section it repealed (Utah Code 20A-19-204 (2019), enacted by Proposition 4 (Justia)). Bill line numbers are shown.

Highlighted Provisions · line 25

repeals existing independent redistricting commission provisions.

The repealer lists all nine Prop 4 sections, 20A-19-101 through 20A-19-301 (S.B. 200 enrolled text).

Maps presented to the Legislature · lines 406–407

The committee or the Legislature may, but is not required to, vote on or adopt a map submitted to the committee or the Legislature by the commission.

(S.B. 200 enrolled text)

Commission standards · lines 354–365

The commission shall define and adopt redistricting standards for use by the commission that require that maps adopted by the commission, to the extent practicable, comply with the following, as defined by the commission: [items (a)–(e) omitted] (f) prohibiting the purposeful or undue favoring or disfavoring of: (i) an incumbent elected official; (ii) a candidate or prospective candidate for elected office; or (iii) a political party.

The standards apply to the commission's maps; the commission defines them (S.B. 200 enrolled text).

Partisan data · lines 366–368

The commission may adopt a standard that prohibits the commission from using any of the following, except for the purpose of conducting an assessment described in Subsection (8): (a) partisan political data;

"May," not "shall" (S.B. 200 enrolled text).

Repealed Prop 4 section 20A-19-204

issue to the public a detailed written report

Required within "seven calendar days after its enactment" if the Legislature passed a map other than the commission's. S.B. 200 repealed this section (S.B. 200 enrolled text; Utah Code 20A-19-204 (2019), enacted by Proposition 4 (Justia)).

The timeline

  1. Nov. 6, 2018 Voters approve Proposition 4, 50.3% to 49.7% (Ballotpedia News, Aug. 29, 2025).
  2. Feb. 27, 2020 S.B. 200 made public, about two weeks before the session ends (S.B. 200 (2020) bill status page).
  3. March 2–11, 2020 Committee hearings in both chambers; passes the Senate 25–0–4 and the House 67–4–4 (S.B. 200 (2020) bill status page).
  4. March 28, 2020 Gov. Gary Herbert signs it (S.B. 200 (2020) bill status page).
  5. July 11, 2024 The Utah Supreme Court rules government-reform initiatives are protected from "unfettered" legislative repeal and sends the case back to district court (Ballotpedia News, Aug. 29, 2025; KJZZ, July 11, 2024, on the Utah Supreme Court ruling).
  6. Aug. 25, 2025 Third District Judge Dianna Gibson rules the repeal of Prop 4 and S.B. 200 unconstitutional and blocks the 2021 congressional map (KUER, Aug. 25, 2025, "Judge tosses out Utah's congressional maps").
  7. Nov. 10, 2025 Judge Gibson picks a plaintiffs' congressional map (FOX 13, Nov. 2025, "Judge picks a new congressional map").
  8. Feb. 20, 2026 The Utah Supreme Court dismisses the Legislature's appeal; the court-chosen map is set for 2026 (Salt Lake Tribune, Feb. 20, 2026).
  9. April 30, 2026 A citizen initiative to repeal Prop 4 falls short in two Senate districts and does not make the ballot (KPCW, May 1, 2026, "Effort to repeal Utah anti-gerrymandering law officially fails").

What happened next

  • The Utah Supreme Court (July 11, 2024, unanimous, Justice Paige Petersen writing) reversed the dismissal of the challenge to the repeal and sent it back to district court (KJZZ, July 11, 2024, on the Utah Supreme Court ruling).
  • On Aug. 25, 2025, Judge Dianna Gibson ruled the repeal and S.B. 200 unconstitutional and blocked the 2021 congressional map (KUER, Aug. 25, 2025, "Judge tosses out Utah's congressional maps"). She later amended the order to remove the requirement that the Legislature redraw the maps (KUER, Aug. 25, 2025, "Judge tosses out Utah's congressional maps").
  • On Nov. 10, 2025, she chose a plaintiffs' map, writing that it "has neither the purpose nor effect of unduly favoring or disfavoring a political party" (FOX 13, Nov. 2025, "Judge picks a new congressional map").
  • On Feb. 20, 2026, the Utah Supreme Court dismissed the Legislature's appeal (Salt Lake Tribune, Feb. 20, 2026). Reps. Burgess Owens and Celeste Maloy had a federal challenge pending at that time (Salt Lake Tribune, Feb. 20, 2026).
  • In 2024, the Legislature also sent Amendment D (S.J.R. 401) to voters, about its power over initiatives; a court voided it (Bill Tracker case file, S.J.R. 401 (Amendment D, 2024)).
  • A 2026 citizen initiative to repeal Prop 4 had 161,961 valid signatures but fell short in two Senate districts (KPCW, May 1, 2026, "Effort to repeal Utah anti-gerrymandering law officially fails").
