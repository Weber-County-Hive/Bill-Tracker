A Water Bill Built on a $1.2 Billion-a-Year Estimate

H.B. 501 would have tied state water money to local rates set by a state formula. It passed the House and died in the Senate.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • H.B. 501 (2026), primary sponsor Rep. Bridger Bolinder, would have made water systems collect a set "local contribution amount" to qualify for state water infrastructure money (H.B. 501 as introduced).
  • The amount was the service area's median adjusted gross income times 1.5% (drinking water) or 3% (drinking water plus wastewater), times the number of connections (H.B. 501 as introduced; 2nd Substitute H.B. 501 (passed the House)).
  • The figures behind it trace to a $170,000 state fee study that found a $1.2 billion yearly need. KSL reported the study did not count revenue water providers already collect (KSL, Oct. 8, 2026, Daniella Rivera; HB 280 Water Infrastructure Projects Fee Study (Zions Public Finance, Oct. 2025)).
  • It passed the House 59–13–3 on Feb. 25, 2026. It never got a Senate committee hearing or floor vote, and died March 6, 2026 (LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).

What the bill says

Quoted from the introduced bill (H.B. 501 as introduced) and the 2nd Substitute that passed the House (2nd Substitute H.B. 501 (passed the House)).

Proposed Utah Code 73-10g-802(1)(a), introduced

to qualify for receipt of state money for water infrastructure or water development

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, a system would have to show it collects at least the local contribution amount (H.B. 501 as introduced).

Proposed Utah Code 73-10g-803(1)(a), introduced

the public water system shall collect from the public water system's customers an amount that equals or exceeds

In the introduced bill this applied from Jan. 1, 2031, whether or not the system sought state money (H.B. 501 as introduced).

Proposed Utah Code 10-8-22(5)(d), introduced

Subsection (5)(b) does not apply to a rate or rate increase that is for the amount needed to pay a fee

(H.B. 501 as introduced)

Proposed Utah Code 73-10g-802(1)(c)(i), 2nd Substitute

subject to applicable requirements set forth in Sections 10-8-22 and 73-10-32.5

The 2nd Substitute kept rates under existing local rate rules (2nd Substitute H.B. 501 (passed the House)).

The timeline

  1. March 18, 2024 H.B. 280 (2024), which required the water infrastructure fee study, is signed (HB 280 Water Infrastructure Projects Fee Study (Zions Public Finance, Oct. 2025); H.B. 280 (2024) Water Related Changes, status page).
  2. October 2025 Zions Public Finance, with Bowen Collins and Associates and Cohne and Kinghorn, completes the fee study: about $1.2 billion a year in water infrastructure expense (HB 280 Water Infrastructure Projects Fee Study (Zions Public Finance, Oct. 2025)).
  3. Feb. 5, 2026 H.B. 501 numbered and introduced (LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).
  4. Feb. 17, 2026 House committee recommends the 1st Substitute 10–2–2 after 13 witnesses (House Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment minutes (1st Sub. H.B. 501); LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).
  5. Feb. 25, 2026 House passes the 2nd Substitute 59–13–3 (LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).
  6. March 3–5, 2026 Senate committee does not consider it; it is circled on Senate 2nd reading (LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).
  7. March 6, 2026 Enacting clause struck; filed as not passed (LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).
  8. Oct. 8, 2026 KSL reports the study did not examine existing revenue; lawmakers are still working on water funding (KSL, Oct. 8, 2026, Daniella Rivera).

What happened next

  • The bill did not pass (LegiScan, H.B. 501 (2026) history and roll calls).
  • KSL reported lawmakers are still working on water funding between sessions (KSL, Oct. 8, 2026, Daniella Rivera).
  • The fee study itself estimates about $852 million a year local agencies could raise through rate increases and a remaining gap of $346 million a year (HB 280 Water Infrastructure Projects Fee Study (Zions Public Finance, Oct. 2025)).
