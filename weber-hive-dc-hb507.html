H.B. 507 / 2026 General Session

The One-Year Gap

H.B. 507 promises to restrict local tax incentives for the largest data centers in Utah. It also builds in a full year, plus a permanent grandfather clause, during which none of that restriction applies — a window that opened four months before Meta announced its latest Eagle Mountain expansion.

Sources: H.B. 507 enrolled text, Server Country policy tracker, Gov. Cox's Executive Order 2026-03 Method: Public records and the bill's own text, no comment requested

A facility has to clear both a 150,000-square-foot minimum and a 100-megawatt threshold to count as a "large load data center" under this bill — a scale limited to the largest hyperscale campuses, not a typical server farm.

From the bill's own definitions, Utah Code §§59-12-102, 54-26-101

What the Bill Actually Restricts

+

Beginning May 6, 2027, political subdivisions are barred from offering new financial incentives to a "large load data center" outside a "Regionally Significant Development Zone" (RSDZ) — the new tax-increment tool this bill creates. Inside an RSDZ, the restriction doesn't apply at all: a data-center-specific zone subtype can capture up to 60% of property tax increment and divert up to 100% of personal property tax revenue, with up to 80% of that usable directly as an incentive. That 100% figure was raised from 80% during the bill's own drafting process, not lowered.

In practice, the bill doesn't close the door on data center incentives. It routes them through a new "Increment Authorization Committee" instead of a simple local vote — a more structured process, but not a smaller one.

The Gap Nobody's Pointed Out

+

The bill itself took effect May 6, 2026. Its own restriction on data-center incentives doesn't start until May 6, 2027 — a full year later. Any incentive agreement signed in that year-long window is grandfathered permanently: it "can continue under its existing terms" forever, outside the new RSDZ review process entirely, so long as it isn't later extended or increased.

Meta announced its latest Eagle Mountain expansion on September 14, 2026 — four months into that window, with eight months still remaining before the restriction takes effect. Whether Eagle Mountain or Utah County has used, or plans to use, that window for this specific expansion isn't established. What is established, directly from the bill's own dates, is that doing so would be entirely legal.

The Governor's Office Already Cited It

+

Gov. Cox's Executive Order 2026-03 — creating a "Data Center Framework" for the Utah Inland Port Authority — explicitly directs UIPA to this bill's own new code sections (17C-6, 63N-3a) as part of the state's response to the Tooele Valley data center controversy on the Great Salt Lake's southern shore. That's real-world confirmation this bill's framework is already in active use, in a live, contested project, not sitting untested.
