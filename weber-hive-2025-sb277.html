Seven Seats Became One

S.B. 277 ended the seven-member State Records Committee that heard public records appeals and gave the job to one director appointed by the governor. The plan to drop GRAMA's balancing test did not survive.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • S.B. 277 (2025), "Government Records Management Amendments," primary sponsor Sen. Michael K. McKell, House sponsor Rep. Jefferson Moss, repealed the State Records Committee and created a Government Records Office inside the Division of Archives and Records Service (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).
  • The office's director, an attorney appointed by the governor with Senate consent, now decides GRAMA appeals the committee used to hear (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).
  • The committee, created in 1992, had seven members, including two citizen representatives and a media representative (Utah News Dispatch, "Utah lawmakers look to dissolve, replace State Records Committee. Here's why that matters," Katie McKellar, Feb. 14, 2025).
  • As introduced, the bill would have removed GRAMA's balancing test. The final law keeps it, with "director" in place of "committee" (Utah News Dispatch, "Utah lawmakers look to dissolve, replace State Records Committee. Here's why that matters," Katie McKellar, Feb. 14, 2025; KUER, "Lawmakers give the green light to dissolve Utah's 7-member State Records Committee," Sean Higgins, March 6, 2025; S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).
  • People can still take the director's decision to district court (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).
  • It passed the Senate 21–8, the House 44–23 and the Senate again 18–6, and was signed March 27, 2025 (LegiScan, S.B. 277 (2025): history and roll calls).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text). Where the bill strikes the words "State Records Committee" and puts in "director," we quote only the words that remain.

General description

replaces the State Records Committee (the committee) with the director of the office,

The director is described as "an attorney with knowledge and experience relating to government records law" (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).

Utah Code 63A-12-202(1)

There is created within the division the Government Records Office.

The division is the Division of Archives and Records Service (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).

Highlighted provisions

requires the governor to appoint the director of the office, in consultation with the executive director of the department, and with the advice and consent of the Senate;

(S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text)

Utah Code 63G-2-403(11)(b), the balancing test

if the public interest favoring access is greater than or equal to the interest favoring restriction of access.

The director may order release of a private, controlled or protected record when this test is met. The bill as introduced would have removed it (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text; Utah News Dispatch, "Utah lawmakers look to dissolve, replace State Records Committee. Here's why that matters," Katie McKellar, Feb. 14, 2025).

Utah Code 63G-2-403(14)

may seek judicial review in district court

A party to a proceeding before the director keeps the right to go to court (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).

The timeline

  1. 1992 The State Records Committee is created and begins hearing appeals when governments deny records requests (Utah News Dispatch, "Utah lawmakers look to dissolve, replace State Records Committee. Here's why that matters," Katie McKellar, Feb. 14, 2025).
  2. Feb. 13, 2025 S.B. 277 numbered (LegiScan, S.B. 277 (2025): history and roll calls).
  3. Feb. 14, 2025 Utah News Dispatch reports the bill would remove the balancing test; the Utah Media Coalition opposes it (Utah News Dispatch, "Utah lawmakers look to dissolve, replace State Records Committee. Here's why that matters," Katie McKellar, Feb. 14, 2025).
  4. Feb. 18, 2025 Senate committee adopts a substitute that keeps the balancing test (LegiScan, S.B. 277 (2025): history and roll calls; "Senate panel advances major change to Utah public records law" (syndicated copy on Yahoo News; outlet and date not shown on the copy read)).
  5. Feb. 27, 2025 Senate passes it 21–8 (LegiScan, S.B. 277 (2025): history and roll calls).
  6. March 5–6, 2025 House passes it 44–23; Senate concurs 18–6 (LegiScan, S.B. 277 (2025): history and roll calls).
  7. March 27, 2025 Gov. Spencer Cox signs it (LegiScan, S.B. 277 (2025): history and roll calls).
  8. June 25, 2025 Senate confirms Lonny Pehrson, records counsel for the Attorney General's Office since 2016, as the first director, 18–8 (Utah News Dispatch via Standard-Examiner, "Utah Senate confirms Gov. Cox's pick for new records director," Katie McKellar, June 25, 2025).
  9. Aug. 20, 2026 The Salt Lake Tribune reports 147 rulings in the director's first 12 months, compared with 74 in the committee's final year (Salt Lake Tribune, "Utah's new public records chief is deciding cases twice as fast as the committee — but not everyone's happy," Robert Gehrke, Aug. 20, 2026).

What happened next

  • Gov. Cox appointed Lonny Pehrson, and the Senate confirmed him 18–8 in an extraordinary session on June 25, 2025 (Utah News Dispatch via Standard-Examiner, "Utah Senate confirms Gov. Cox's pick for new records director," Katie McKellar, June 25, 2025).
  • The Salt Lake Tribune reported on Aug. 20, 2026 that the director was on pace to hear appeals within 64 days and rule within 73, compared with six months or more under the committee; 372 appeals had been received (Salt Lake Tribune, "Utah's new public records chief is deciding cases twice as fast as the committee — but not everyone's happy," Robert Gehrke, Aug. 20, 2026).
  • The same report found appellants won fully or partly about one-third of the time under the director, compared with 40% in the committee's last three years, and noted the committee data set is small (Salt Lake Tribune, "Utah's new public records chief is deciding cases twice as fast as the committee — but not everyone's happy," Robert Gehrke, Aug. 20, 2026).
  • Pending committee cases were transferred to the director, and the committee's rules stay in use until the director makes new ones (S.B. 277 (2025) enrolled text).
