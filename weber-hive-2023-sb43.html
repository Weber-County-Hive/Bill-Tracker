Hearing Notices Leave the Newspaper

S.B. 43 created "class A" and "class B" public notices. For initiative hearings, a newspaper notice by sponsors became a county clerk's class A notice, and clerks may bill sponsors for it.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Part of Ten Years of Petition Laws, 2016–2026: 35 changes to Utah's initiative and referendum rules, every one written and passed by the Legislature. S.B. 43 (2023) is one of them.

Short version

  • S.B. 43 (2023), primary sponsor Sen. Stephanie Pitcher, House sponsor Rep. Norman K. Thurston, "creates classifications for types of public notices" and changed notice rules across state law (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).
  • For the public hearings held before a statewide initiative circulates, the newspaper notice that sponsors had to publish was replaced by a county clerk's class A notice for at least three days (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).
  • New in the same section: "A county clerk may bill the sponsors of the initiative petition for the cost" of that notice (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).
  • Its own text raised sponsors' advance notice from three to seven days; H.B. 38, passed the same session, set it at 10, and the 2023 code reads 10 (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page); Utah Code 20A-7-204.1, 2023 copy (Justia); H.B. 38 (2023) enrolled text).
  • It also moved the Lieutenant Governor's publication of proposed constitutional amendments from "at least one newspaper in every county" to a class A notice for the state (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page); Utah Code 20A-7-103, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • Final votes were House 67–0–8 and Senate 26–0–3 after a conference committee; Gov. Cox signed it March 20, 2023 (Bill page and status (Chapter 435); House final passage roll call, Feb. 23, 2023; Senate final passage roll call, Feb. 24, 2023).

What the bill says

Quoted from the enrolled bill (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)). Text in [brackets] was struck.

Highlighted provisions (lines 13–14)

creates classifications for types of public notices where each classification requires notice to be provided in specific ways;

(S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page))

Utah Code 20A-7-204.1(2)(b) (lines 7406–7410)

A county clerk who receives a notice from a sponsor under Subsection (2)(a) shall publish written notice of the public hearing[, including the time, date, and location of the public hearing, in each county in the region where the public hearing will be held:] for the county, as a class A notice under Section 63G-28-102, for at least three days before the day of the public hearing.

(S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page))

Utah Code 20A-7-204.1(2)(c) (lines 7411–7412)

A county clerk may bill the sponsors of the initiative petition for the cost of preparing, printing, and publishing the notice required under Subsection (2)(b).

(S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page))

Struck from 20A-7-204.1 (lines 7413–7414)

[(i) (A) at least three calendar days before the day of the public hearing, in a newspaper of general circulation in the county;]

Also struck: posting copies where there is no newspaper, mailing to each residence, the Utah Public Notice Website, the legal-notice law, and the county website, each listed separately (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).

Utah Code 20A-7-204.1(2)(a) (line 7398)

before 5 p.m. at least [three] seven calendar days before the date of the public hearing

Sponsors' notice to the Lieutenant Governor, lawmakers, county officials and now each county clerk in the region (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).

Utah Code 20A-7-103(2) (lines 7349–7352)

publish the full text of the amendment, question, or statute [in at least one newspaper in every county of the state where a newspaper is published] for the state, as a class A notice under Section 63G-28-102, through the date of the election.

The Lieutenant Governor's duty for proposed constitutional amendments (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).

Utah Code 63G-28-102(1)(a), new (line 9484)

shall publish the public notice on the Utah Public Notice Website;

A class A notice also goes on the body's own website if its budget is $250,000 or more, and is posted in a public place in the area (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).

Utah Code 63G-28-102(4)(a), new (lines 9537–9539)

is not required to comply with Subsection (1)(c) if: (a) the affected area is the state;

For a statewide notice, posting in a public place is not required (S.B. 43 (2023) enrolled text (publisher's pasted copy of the le.utah.gov page)).

The timeline

  1. Jan. 3, 2023 S.B. 43 numbered and made public (Bill page and status (Chapter 435)).
  2. Jan. 20, 2023 Senate adopts a 1st Substitute and passes it 26–0–3 (Bill page and status (Chapter 435)).
  3. Jan. 26, 2023 House committee recommends it, 9–1–2 (Bill page and status (Chapter 435)).
  4. Feb. 15, 2023 House adopts Rep. Thurston's 2nd Substitute and passes it 69–0–6 (Bill page and status (Chapter 435)).
  5. Feb. 22–24, 2023 Senate and House disagree; a conference committee reports 6–0–0; final passage 67–0–8 and 26–0–3 (Bill page and status (Chapter 435); House final passage roll call, Feb. 23, 2023; Senate final passage roll call, Feb. 24, 2023).
  6. March 20, 2023 Gov. Cox signs it (Bill page and status (Chapter 435)).
  7. May 3, 2023 S.B. 43 and H.B. 38 take effect. The 2023 code for initiative hearings lists sponsors' notice at 10 days and the clerk's class A notice; it has no newspaper line (Bill page and status (Chapter 435); Utah Code 20A-7-204.1, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  8. Sept. 12, 2024 A district court voids Amendment D. Utah News Dispatch reports one ground was that state officials "failed to meet publication requirements" (Utah News Dispatch, Katie McKellar, Oct. 24, 2024 (Amendment D)).
  9. Oct. 24, 2024 The Utah Supreme Court upholds that decision (Utah News Dispatch, Katie McKellar, Oct. 24, 2024 (Amendment D)).
  10. Nov. 3, 2026 Amendment A (H.J.R. 10) is on the ballot; it would remove the Constitution's newspaper rule for proposed amendments (Weber County Hive: The Amendments on Your Ballot, Part 1).

In their own words

Quoted exactly from the source named. A quote does not tell you everything a person thinks; it is what the record shows they said.

published in at least one newspaper in every county of the state

— The Utah Constitution's rule for proposed amendments, as quoted by Utah News Dispatch, Utah News Dispatch, Oct. 24, 2024 (Utah News Dispatch, Katie McKellar, Oct. 24, 2024 (Amendment D))

What happened next

  • In the 2023 code, sponsors also give notice of each hearing "in accordance with Section 45-1-101," the legal-notice law (Utah Code 20A-7-204.1, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • That law requires legal notices to go "on a public legal notice website established by the combined efforts of Utah's newspapers" (Utah Code 45-1-101, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • The 2023 code bills sponsors for "preparing, printing, and posting the notice," and cites the class A rule as Section 63G-30-102 (Utah Code 20A-7-204.1, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • The 2023 code for constitutional amendments reads "for the state, as a class A notice," with a history note "Amended by Chapter 435, 2023 General Session" (S.B. 43) (Utah Code 20A-7-103, 2023 copy (Justia)).
  • In 2024, Amendment D was voided; one ground reported was the newspaper publication requirement (Utah News Dispatch, Katie McKellar, Oct. 24, 2024 (Amendment D)).

What voters are told

    rules for arguments for and against ballot measures
  2. 2017 S.B. 69 B−
    voters must be told about local ballot measures
  3. 2022 S.B. 38 C+
    a short ballot title and summary replaced the long title
  4. 2023 H.B. 69 C
    dropped the separate ballot measure insert when the same information is on the ballot
  5. 2023 S.B. 43 C−
    pre-circulation hearing notices for initiatives moved from the newspaper to class A notice
  6. 2025 Amendment A (H.J.R. 10)
    on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot: would remove the Constitution's rule that proposed amendments be printed in a newspaper in every county, and let the Legislature set the method by statute

Where it ended up: S.B. 43 moved initiative hearing notices out of the newspaper by statute in 2023. Amendment A would make the same kind of move for constitutional amendments, in the Constitution itself. In 2024, the missing newspaper notice was one of the two reasons the Utah Supreme Court gave for voiding Amendment D. Amendment A is not one of the 35 petition laws and is not graded here; see The Amendments on Your Ballot, Part 1.
