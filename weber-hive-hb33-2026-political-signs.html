CASE 2026-033

H.B. 33 · 2026 General Session · Political Signs Amendments

Smaller Exemption, New Exemption

H.B. 33 shrank the size of campaign signs that can skip a "paid for by" line, from 4-by-8 feet down to under 24-by-18 inches. The Senate's version, adopted by voice vote, added a second exemption for any sign a candidate pays for that names that candidate, and dropped a rule that the "paid for by" line be readable up close.

Primary Sponsor: Rep. Jordan D. Teuscher · Senate Sponsor: Sen. Evan J. Vickers Effective: Signed March 23, 2026 Became Law

Posted Nov. 26, 2026 · Updated Nov. 26, 2026

New Here? Start Simple

Most campaign ads must say who paid for them. Some signs get a pass.

Before 2026, Utah law let any lawn sign 4 by 8 feet or smaller skip the "paid for by" line. That covers nearly every yard sign.

H.B. 33 cut that pass down to signs smaller than 24 by 18 inches. So most yard signs now need a "paid for by" line.

But the final version added a new pass: a sign of any size does not need the line if the candidate's own campaign paid for it and the sign clearly names that candidate.

The bill also made new rules about covering up or removing other people's signs, signs on utility poles, and where removed signs go.

Tried Before: H.B. 292 (2025)

Teuscher brought a similar bill a year earlier. H.B. 292 (2025), also called "Political Signs Amendments," would have cut the sign exemption from 4 by 8 feet to 24 by 18 inches and banned signs on utility poles. Sen. Vickers was its Senate sponsor.

It passed the House 69–0–6 on Feb. 18, 2025, and a Senate committee recommended it 4–0–3. It never got a Senate floor vote. It was filed as "not passed" at 11:59 p.m. on March 7, 2025, the last night of the session.

Before the 2026 session, the Government Operations Interim Committee recommended the new bill, H.B. 33, by a vote of 13 to 1, with 3 absent (committee note on the bill).

The Votes

The House committee recommended the bill 11–0–1, and the House passed it 72–0–3 on Jan. 23, 2026. The Senate committee recommended it 7–0–0, and the Senate passed it on second reading 27–0–2.

On Feb. 12, 2026, at 11:03 a.m., the Senate replaced the bill with its 1st Substitute by voice vote. That is the version that added the candidate-paid exemption and dropped the up-close rule. It passed 27–0–2 seven minutes later. The House agreed to the Senate's version 69–1–5 on Feb. 13. Gov. Cox signed it March 23, 2026.

How This Could Have Been Clearer

  • A roll-call vote when the Senate swapped in its 1st Substitute
  • A plain statement in the bill summary that the up-close readability rule was removed
  • Keeping a minimum type size for the "paid for by" line on ordinary signs

Three Ways to Look at It

An auditor's look: All bill versions and votes are posted. The Senate version added the candidate-paid pass and dropped the up-close rule. It was adopted by voice vote. On the money side, $35,682 paid to Invencio is labeled only "Advertising" or "Campaign Expense." The reports don't show what it bought.

The Legislature's look: Lawmakers voted almost all yes at every recorded step. The bill changed which signs must say who paid. It also added new rules for taking signs down.

The collective look: Most yard signs now need a "paid for by" line. That helps voters. Signs paid for by a candidate that name the candidate do not. And the line no longer has to be readable up close.

What could have been done differently: a roll call on the Senate version, and a smallest-type-size rule for the "paid for by" line.

Questions to Ask, and Who to Ask

  1. Why was the up-close readability rule dropped in the Senate version? Ask members of the Senate Government Operations and Political Subdivisions Committee, or listen to the Feb. 12, 2026 Senate floor audio on the bill page.
  2. Where can I drop off a sign I removed, and how many signs has my city stored? Ask your city recorder or county clerk.
  3. What did the $35,682 paid to Invencio buy? Campaign reports list only "Advertising" or "Campaign Expense." Ask the Lieutenant Governor's Elections Office whether more detail is required.

Words to Know

  • Disclosure statement: The "paid for by" line on an ad that tells you who paid for it.
  • Infraction: The lowest level of offense in Utah. It carries a fine but no jail.
  • Class B misdemeanor: A crime that can carry up to six months in jail and a fine.
  • Park strip: The strip of land between a street and a sidewalk.
  • Substitute: A full replacement version of a bill, adopted before a vote.
