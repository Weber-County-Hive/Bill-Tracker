2012: The Year Utah Wrote Online Gambling Into Its Ban

Two 2012 bills, H.B. 108 and H.B. 40, laid the first pieces of the gambling toolbox Utah used in 2026. One wrote online gambling into the ban and promised to opt out of any federal law allowing it. The other tightened the rule on store promotions.

The story in 5 steps

1. The goal in 2012Utah lawmakers wanted it written into the law that gambling on the internet or a phone is still gambling here, and that Utah would say no if Congress ever allowed it.
2. What H.B. 108 didIt said gambling includes "any Internet or online gambling." It made offering online gambling to anyone in Utah a Class A misdemeanor. And it said if Congress ever allows online gambling and lets states opt out, Utah "shall opt out."
3. The internet-provider fightThe first day in committee, lawmakers added a rule making internet providers block gambling sites. The next day they took it out. The final law says providers aren't guilty just for carrying the traffic.
4. What H.B. 40 didStores can run promotions, but not ones that are really gambling. H.B. 40 tightened that line and listed six things to weigh. Layton City's attorney's office helped present it.
5. Why it matters nowIn 2020 the online-gambling crime became a felony. In 2026 Utah charged Kalshi with 12 felony counts of it. The 2012 bill started that crime.

Every step is backed by the records further down this page (H.B. 108 (2012) enrolled (final) text; House Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 7, 2012; House Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 8, 2012; H.B. 40 (2012) enrolled (final) text; House Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Jan. 24, 2012; State of Utah v. KalshiEX, LLC, Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, Criminal Information and Declaration of Probable Cause, Third District Court, Salt Lake County, dated Oct. 28, 2026 (AGO No. 9465862), posted by Utah News Dispatch).

The toolbox, piece by piece

In simple words: Utah didn't build its 2026 case against betting apps in one year. Each of these laws added a piece.

Short version

  • H.B. 108, "Internet Gambling" (Rep. Stephen Sandstrom; Sen. John Valentine): a person is guilty of gambling who "participates in gambling, including any Internet or online gambling." Anyone who "intentionally provides or offers to provide any form of Internet or online gambling to any person in this state" is guilty of a Class A misdemeanor (H.B. 108 (2012) enrolled (final) text).
  • It says that if a federal law allows online gambling and lets states opt out, "this state shall opt out," and that the section stands as Utah's ban whether or not such a federal law passes (H.B. 108 (2012) enrolled (final) text).
  • Internet providers, hosting companies, phone companies and ad services are not guilty just for carrying or storing the material (H.B. 108 (2012) enrolled (final) text).
  • H.B. 40, "Gambling Amendments" (Rep. Don Ipson; Sen. Stephen Urquhart), recommended by the Judiciary interim committee: a store promotion is exempt from "fringe gambling" only if it is "clearly ancillary to the primary activity of a business," judged by six listed factors (H.B. 40 (2012) enrolled (final) text; H.B. 40 (2012) Gambling Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).
  • H.B. 108 passed the House 61–9–5 and the Senate 25–2–2, and took effect July 1, 2012. H.B. 40 passed 72–1–2 and 27–0–2, and took effect May 8, 2012 (House 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 21, 2012 (61-9-5); Senate 3rd reading roll call, Mar. 1, 2012 (25-2-2); H.B. 108 (2012) Internet Gambling, bill page and status, le.utah.gov; House 3rd reading roll call on H.B. 40, Jan. 31, 2012 (72-1-2); Senate 3rd reading roll call on H.B. 40, Feb. 10, 2012 (27-0-2); H.B. 40 (2012) Gambling Amendments, bill page and status, le.utah.gov).

What they say

Quoted from the final bills (H.B. 108 (2012) enrolled (final) text; H.B. 40 (2012) enrolled (final) text).

H.B. 108: the online-gambling crime (then 76-10-1102(3))

A person is guilty of a class A misdemeanor who intentionally provides or offers to provide any form of Internet or online gambling to any person in this state.

H.B. 108: the federal opt-out (then 76-10-1102(4)–(5))

If any federal law is enacted that authorizes Internet gambling in the states and that federal law provides that individual states may opt out of Internet gambling, this state shall opt out of Internet gambling ...

And: "Whether or not any federal law is enacted that authorizes Internet gambling in the states, this section acts as this state's prohibition of any gambling, including Internet gambling, in this state."

H.B. 108: what "online gambling" means

"Internet gambling" or "online gambling" means gambling or gaming by use of: (a) the Internet; or (b) any mobile electronic device that allows access to data and information.

H.B. 40: the store-promotion line

"Fringe gambling" does not mean a promotional activity that is clearly ancillary to the primary activity of a business.

It replaced "clearly occasional and ancillary" and added six factors to weigh, such as how the business is marketed and whether buyers get a better chance to win than people who don't buy. H.B. 23 removed the six factors in 2019 (see H.B. 23).

How H.B. 108 changed

In simple words: the House committee went back and forth on whether internet companies should have to block gambling sites. In the end, they don't.

WhenWhat happened
Feb. 7, 2012, House committeeAdopted an amendment saying an internet provider in Utah "shall apply available technology to intercept, block, or otherwise prevent" gambling traffic. Then voted 9–3–1 to hold the bill (House Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 7, 2012; House committee vote to hold H.B. 108, Feb. 7, 2012 (9-3-1))
Feb. 8, 2012, House committeeStruck that amendment. Adopted another letting police use technology to disable gambling traffic and asking providers to help, with police paying the cost. Recommended the bill 10–0–3 (House Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice Committee minutes, Feb. 8, 2012; House committee favorable vote, Feb. 8, 2012 (10-0-3))
Feb. 21, 2012, House floorFloor amendment (voice vote); passed 61–9–5 (House 3rd reading roll call, Feb. 21, 2012 (61-9-5))
Feb. 24, 2012, Senate committeeSen. Bramble's amendment to the gambling section adopted; recommended 7–0–1 (Senate Business and Labor Committee minutes, Feb. 24, 2012; Senate committee favorable vote, Feb. 24, 2012 (7-0-1))
Final lawNeither the blocking rule nor the Feb. 8 police-technology rule is in it. Internet providers, hosting companies, phone companies and ad services are exempt when they only carry or store the material (H.B. 108 (2012) enrolled (final) text)
