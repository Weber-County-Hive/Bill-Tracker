Who Asked for What: 2026 Requests for Appropriation

Every request a legislator filed to get state money for a project in 2026, sorted by who asked, which pot of money it would come from, and which committee heard it.

Important: these are requests, not final spending. A request for appropriation (RFA) is a legislator asking for money. Being on this list does not mean the money was approved. The Legislature's list labels its date column "Date approved," but the list itself is of requests. Whether any one request was funded shows up in the final appropriations bills, not here.

New here? The short version

What is an RFA? In Utah, a legislator who wants state money for a program or project files a "request for appropriation." House requests are numbered H.A.R., Senate requests S.A.R. An appropriations subcommittee hears each one.

Where would the money come from?

  • General Fund (GF): mostly sales tax. Pays for most of state government.
  • Income Tax Fund (ITF): income tax money. Constitutionally tied to education and certain social services.
  • Uniform School Fund (USF): money set aside for public schools.
  • Other: federal money, restricted accounts and other sources.

One-time vs. ongoing. One-time money is spent once. Ongoing money is built into the budget and repeats every year unless lawmakers remove it. The "total" figures on this page add one-time money plus one year of ongoing money.

The big picture

A few requests appear to be filed twice by different sponsors (marked possible overlap below). Totals on this page count every line exactly as the Legislature listed it, so those requests are counted twice.

Funded without a request by that name: the Pioneer Trail

Up to $10 million for the Pioneer Trail Foundation is written into H.B. 2 (2026) across four lines: $5,000,000 in item 27 (Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity), up to $2,000,000 in item 148 (Natural Resources), up to $1,000,000 in item 170 (Capital Improvements) and up to $2,000,000 in item 179 (Transportation).

We searched this 2026 request list (copied Oct. 4, 2026) for "Pioneer," "Capitol" and "Council Hall" and found no request under those search terms. A request could exist under another title. News reports say Gov. Cox's budget asked for $5 million.

Questions to ask, and who to ask

  1. Was this request funded, and for how much? Ask the sponsoring legislator, or check the Legislative Fiscal Analyst's budget site (COBI), which tracks each request.
  2. Who receives the money? Many titles name a program, not a recipient. Ask the sponsor which organization or agency gets the funds.
  3. For ongoing money: how will results be measured? Ongoing money repeats every year. Ask the sponsor or the appropriations subcommittee chair what reporting is required.
  4. Why are some requests filed by two sponsors? Ask either sponsor whether the overlapping requests are the same project, so the totals are not counted twice.
  5. Does the sponsor have a connection to the recipient? Legislators' conflict-of-interest forms are public on the House and Senate websites.
