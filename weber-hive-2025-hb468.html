Rules for License Plate Readers

H.B. 468 (2025) would have set statewide rules for how government uses license plate reader cameras: a 90-day limit on keeping data, a warrant to track a known vehicle with private camera data, yearly reports and penalties. It passed the House 64–2 and a Senate committee 6–0, then died on the last night of the 2025 session.

Short version

  • H.B. 468 (2025), "Automatic License Plate Reader Amendments," primary sponsor Rep. Kristen Chevrier, would have rewritten Utah's rules for government use of license plate reader (ALPR) cameras (H.B. 468 (2025) bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov; H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray)).
  • Every version we read keeps plate data at least 14 days and requires it to be deleted within 90 days, unless it is needed for a criminal case or a civil enforcement action (H.B. 468 (2025) as introduced; H.B. 468 1st Substitute (Buff); H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray)).
  • The House floor amendments required a warrant before police use data from a privately operated reader to locate or track a known vehicle, with an exception for emergencies (2nd Sub. H.B. 468, House Amendment 2 (Feb. 25, 2025); 2nd Sub. H.B. 468, House Amendment 3 (Feb. 26, 2025)).
  • Police agencies would have run a yearly internal audit and reported to the state; misuse would be a class B misdemeanor (H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray)).
  • It passed the House 64–2–9 on Feb. 27, 2025 and a Senate committee 6–0–2 on March 4 (H.B. 468 (2025) bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov). It was sent to Senate Rules on March 5 and never got a Senate floor vote. Its enacting clause was struck at 11:59 p.m. on March 7, 2025, the last night (H.B. 468 (2025) bill data record (actions, times, vote IDs), le.utah.gov).

What the bill says

Quoted from the 2nd Substitute, the version the House amended and passed, and from House Amendment 2 (H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray); 2nd Sub. H.B. 468, House Amendment 2 (Feb. 25, 2025)). We did not locate the final amended text as one document.

Utah Code 41-6a-2004(1)

delete or destroy the captured plate data within 90 days from the date of capture

Data must also be kept at least 14 days. Longer retention is allowed for criminal evidence or an ongoing civil enforcement action (H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray)).

Utah Code 41-6a-2005(5) (House Amendment 2)

only pursuant to a warrant issued using the procedures described in the Utah Rules of Criminal Procedure or an equivalent federal warrant

Applies when police use data from a privately operated reader to locate or track a known vehicle (2nd Sub. H.B. 468, House Amendment 2 (Feb. 25, 2025)).

Utah Code 41-6a-2003.3(2)(c)

conduct an annual internal audit to ensure compliance with this part

Applies to law enforcement agencies (H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray)).

Utah Code 41-6a-2006(1)

or captured license plate data in violation of this part is guilty

...of a class B misdemeanor, for someone who knowingly or intentionally misuses the system or its data (H.B. 468 2nd Substitute (Gray)).

What happened next
