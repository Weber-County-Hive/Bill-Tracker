Posting Who Signed a Candidate's Petition

H.B. 167 would have posted the names of voters who sign a candidate's nomination petition and sent forgery complaints to prosecutors. A House committee held it 5–2 on Feb. 3; it never moved again.

Posted Oct. 19, 2026 · Updated Oct. 19, 2026

Short version

  • H.B. 167 (2026), "Signature Collection Modifications," primary sponsor Rep. Michael L. Kohler, was about petitions candidates use to get on the ballot (H.B. 167 as introduced).
  • Election officials would have posted the name and signing date of each verified signer on the lieutenant governor's website for at least 90 days after the primary (H.B. 167 1st Substitute (Buff)).
  • If someone said they never signed, the election official would have had to tell the county or district attorney (H.B. 167 as introduced; H.B. 167 1st Substitute (Buff)).
  • House Government Operations held it on Feb. 3, 2026, 5–2–5 (LegiScan, H.B. 167 (2026); The Weber County Hive, Bills That Did the Opposite, Part 4 (11:59 p.m., March 6)). On March 2 the committee returned it to Rules with no vote shown (House Government Operations Committee report, March 2, 2026 (returned to Rules)).
  • Its enacting clause was struck on March 6, 2026, when unfinished bills were closed out (LegiScan, H.B. 167 (2026); The Weber County Hive, Bills That Did the Opposite, Part 4 (11:59 p.m., March 6)).

What the bill says

Quoted from the 1st Substitute, the last version (H.B. 167 1st Substitute (Buff)), and the introduced bill (H.B. 167 as introduced).

Utah Code 20A-9-403(3)(d)(vi)

post the individual's name and date of signature on the lieutenant governor's website

Within five business days after the signature is verified, for at least 90 days after the primary election (H.B. 167 1st Substitute (Buff)).

Utah Code 20A-9-405(10)

notifies the filing officer that the person did not sign the nomination petition

When that happens, the filing officer notifies the county or district attorney (H.B. 167 1st Substitute (Buff)).

Utah Code 20A-9-405(11)(a)

no later than 5 p.m. three business days after the day on which:

The deadline for a signer to ask to have their signature removed (H.B. 167 1st Substitute (Buff)).

What happened next

  • H.B. 167 did not become law (LegiScan, H.B. 167 (2026)).
  • For initiative and referendum petitions (not candidate petitions), H.B. 32 (2026) did pass: from July 1, 2027, clerks tell signers whether their signature counted.
